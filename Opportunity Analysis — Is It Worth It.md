# Opportunity Analysis — Is this worth our time?

*Companion to "Market Research & Strategy.md" and the interactive "Opportunity & Pricing Model.xlsx." This is the money question, answered against your real numbers: Ray $55/hr (but only ~$40k last year), Pierce $15/hr (firefighter, off ~4 days/week), Chase $18/hr (escape-room game guide).*

---

## Short answer: yes — and it's not close

The reason isn't that these services pay a great hourly wage. It's that **you stop being paid a wage at all and start keeping the margin.** That's the whole game.

- As a **wage earner**, Ray's ceiling is **$55/hr × hours**. Last year that capped out at ~**$40k** because the hours weren't there.
- As an **owner**, Ray keeps the **margin the business makes above labor** — and that scales with the *crew*, not just his two hands.

When you run the actual unit economics (full detail in the model), every single service nets the *business* far more per hour than the brothers' wage — which means **you can pay Pierce and Chase more than they make now and still keep a fat margin on top.**

## Your "one-stop front door" model — and how to keep the money straight

You nailed the structure: **OBX Lot Solutions is the front door** — *"We handle almost everything your property needs"* — and **Jamieson Automation is the specialist subcontractor** for the smart-home/tech side. Over time you bolt on more (a licensed electrician partner for EV/generators, a painter, etc.) and OBX becomes the single number a property manager calls for *anything*.

Why this works: property managers and absentee owners **desperately want fewer vendors.** One reliable call that handles cleaning, washing, watching, junk, *and* tech is worth more to them than five separate contractors — especially when reliable help is scarce here.

**One critical money rule so this doesn't quietly cost you:** you own **100% of Jamieson** but only **1/3 of OBX Lot Solutions.** So when OBX sells a job that includes tech work, **Jamieson must invoice for the tech portion** (either OBX pays Jamieson as a subcontractor, or the customer pays Jamieson directly for that line). If tech revenue just lands inside OBX, you've handed 2/3 of your premium margin to your partners. Keep the cleanup money in OBX and the tech money in Jamieson. The app already keeps the two businesses' books separate — use that.

## The honest risks (so this is a real decision, not a pitch)

- **Labor reliability.** The whole OBX model leans on Pierce and Chase showing up. Pierce's firefighter schedule actually helps (4 days off), but build in redundancy and don't take same-day weekend rental turnovers until the crew is proven.
- **Your time is the bottleneck.** Jamieson pays 3× per hour, but only while *you* work it. The fix is to run OBX on the brothers so it earns without you, freeing your hours for Jamieson — exactly the two-business split you've built.
- **Seasonality.** OBX revenue swings hard with tourism. Counter it with **recurring** contracts (house-watch, recurring washing) and the **Q4 holiday-lighting** spike that lands in the dead season.
- **Cash to start.** Most of these are low-startup (you own the washer; house-watch needs nothing). The trailer for junk and a permanent-lighting demo are the main early spends.
