Hand-troweled plaster around the opening

Built exactly like the living-room side: the plaster wraps the opening, returns to the edge of the glass on both sides and across the top, hides the bronze frame and the basin, and has the light in the top. Same box, different skin. Three looks: tadelakt is Moroccan lime plaster burnished to a soft sheen and sealed so it shrugs off splash; Roman clay is matte and velvety with visible trowel movement; limewash is the quietest, chalky and pale, and nearly disappears behind your pottery.

Good to know: plaster is done by hand, so the movement in it is the point. We do a sample board in your room before the first coat goes on.

Included on both sides
  • Light bars at the top, living room and dining room: warm LED strips tucked into the top reveal, washing down the glass and the finish. Plug-in, no new wiring.
  • The pump outlet stays reachable. It ends up behind the new surround, so one tile panel at the foot of the living-room side lifts off with magnets. Nothing is buried.
  • We check the pump circuit. A pump sitting in water should be on a GFCI-protected circuit. We test it before we close anything up and tell you if it is not.
  • The waterfall itself is untouched. We build around it, never into it. The basin stays open so the pump comes out from above, and the joint where the finish meets the glass and the bronze frame is sealed with a flexible bead.
  • About 6 working days on site, with the water off for about 3 of them while the tile and plaster cure and the joints are sealed.
