Dining room side · Roman clay

Hand-troweled Roman clay around the opening

Built exactly like the living-room side: the surround wraps the opening, returns to the edge of the glass on both sides and across the top, hides the bronze frame and the basin, and has the light in the top. Roman clay is matte and velvety with visible trowel movement, and it carries straight onto the wall and into the pottery niches, so the surround, the columns and the niches are one continuous finish. Sample on the wall before the first coat.

Included on both sides
  • Light bars at the top, living room and dining room: warm LED strips tucked into the top reveal, washing down the glass and the finish. Plug-in, no new wiring.
  • The pump outlet stays reachable. It ends up behind the new surround, so one tile panel at the foot of the living-room side lifts off with magnets. Nothing is buried.
  • The pump outlet gets GFCI protection. A pump sitting in water belongs on a GFCI-protected outlet, so the upgrade is on the quote and goes in before anything is closed up.
  • The waterfall itself is untouched. We build around it, never into it. The basin stays open so the pump comes out from above, and the joint where the finish meets the glass and the bronze frame is sealed with a flexible bead.
  • About 11 working days on site, with the water off for about 3 of them while the tile and plaster cure and the joints are sealed.

Both columns get the same Roman clay as the waterfall, so the whole wall reads as one piece: the column faces, the pottery niches behind and beside the shelves, and the plaster reveals inside the arches. The live-edge shelves come off first and go back on when the clay has cured, so the niche walls are worked as open wall and the shelves stay wood. While they are down, the LED wiring for each shelf is re-terminated properly and the stripped mounting screws are replaced, so the shelves go back on solid. The quatrefoil tile inside the arches stays as it is. The ten wall patches are skimmed twice, sanded and primed first, because Roman clay shows any change in texture underneath it.

Areas are estimated from one measured column (43.5 by 98 inch faces, 25.5 by 97 inch sides) and the photos. We measure everything on day one, and the price holds unless the measured area differs by more than 10 percent.
