# Scaled Operators — Growth-Lever Research

*How home-watch and smart-home install businesses grew past the solo founder. Named examples with the real numbers and the expansion mechanic behind each. Purpose: a menu of growth levers for Jamieson Automation's own plan — Ray's scarce skilled time is the bottleneck, so the question is always "how did they grow without the founder doing every install?"*

**Draft — research brief. Figures are from public sources (cited); franchise-system averages are flagged as such and are not the same as a single independent operator's take-home.**

---

## The headline for Ray

The two relevant industries — **home watch** and **custom smart-home integration** — are both fragmented, mostly solo or small, and both are converging on the *same* model Jamieson is already built around: **install once, then bill a recurring monitoring/management fee.** The operators who broke past solo did it with one of four levers, not heroics:

1. **Productize + systematize** so non-founder labor can deliver (checklists, standard packages, reporting software).
2. **Recurring revenue** (monitoring/management fees) to smooth the seasonal cash and fund payroll.
3. **A channel that delivers volume** (builders, property managers, an association/referral network) instead of one-off chasing.
4. **Franchise or multi-territory** once the playbook is documented.

Jamieson already has #1 and #2 in motion (the J-Suite app, the packages, the managed plan). The research says the next unlock is **#3 — a volume channel** — which is exactly the PM / Second-Home-Hub motion.

## Home-watch operators

### Bill Wright — Wright Home Watch Services (Charlotte & Sarasota counties, FL)
- **The story:** Started 2012. Grew **from a handful of clients to 200+ by end of 2018**; has personally provided/consulted/managed **10,000+ home-watch service calls.**
- **The lever:** Customer service + a **proprietary comprehensive reporting system** — i.e. he productized the deliverable so it was consistent and trustable, which is what let it scale beyond him. He then founded the **Florida Home Watch Association** (2014), turning his playbook into an industry-standard and a referral/credibility engine.
- **Read-across for Jamieson:** the reporting system = our install checklist + warranty tracker + photo handoff. Standardized, branded documentation is itself a growth lever — it makes the service repeatable by someone other than the founder and builds referral trust. (Ray's brothers / a future hire deliver to a checklist; Ray isn't the only one who can do a visit.)

### Homewatch CareGivers — national franchise (benchmark, not an independent)
- **The numbers:** Franchise **average gross revenue ~$632,000/yr**; net profit typically **10–15%**, i.e. **~$63k–$93k/yr per franchise.** Franchising since **2002**, now **33 states + international.**
- **Caveat:** this brand straddles home-watch and in-home *care*, so treat the revenue as a directional ceiling for a single well-run territory, not a pure home-watch figure.
- **The lever:** documented playbook → multi-territory franchising; many run **semi-absentee / manager-run**, meaning the owner isn't the one doing the work. That's the end-state proof that this service *can* be run without the founder on every job.
- **Read-across:** the path from "Ray does everything" to "Ray runs a crew/territory" is well-trodden. The unlock is a documented, teachable system (which we're building) + a manager layer.

### National Home Watch Association (context)
- Market tailwind: ~**7.15M second homes** in the US (NAHB, 2020), and roughly **two of every three wealthy Americans own a second home.** Snowbirds, seasonal owners, business travelers, renovation clients all in the addressable pool. The OBX is dense with exactly this customer.
- The industry is explicitly trending toward **adding smart sensors / remote monitoring on top of physical visits** for continuous coverage and **layered recurring fees** — validation of Jamieson's whole thesis and the Second-Home Hub angle.

## Smart-home / custom-integration operators

### Smart Systems Technologies (Southern California & Arizona)
- **Scale:** Ranked **#10 on the CE Pro 100** (the industry's top-integrators list — these firms run from tens of millions in annual revenue at the top end). A total low-voltage integrator with a custom division.
- **The lever — channel volume via builders:** instead of hunting one-off homeowners, they embedded with **residential tract builders**, deploying a standardized smart-home base package across **communities of 40–100 homes**, then capturing **~90% upgrade rates** (surround sound, Sonos, network upgrades, cameras) on top.
- **The mechanic:** a base package in every home (predictable, repeatable, crew-delivered) + a sales motion that meets each buyer to sell upgrades. White-glove tech handoff (load apps, train the homeowner) — *the same handoff step we built into the install checklist.*
- **Read-across for Jamieson:** **this is the single most important model in the brief.** Swap "tract builder" for "**property-management company**" or "**the Second-Home Care Hub's owner base**" and it's Jamieson's exact play — one channel partner delivering many doors, a standard package in each, upgrades captured on top. It's how you escape one-at-a-time selling. The PM pitch and per-door package we already built are the front end of this.

### Custom-integration industry shape (context)
- The CE Pro 100 is full of firms that **started small and grew into multi-million-dollar businesses** via organic growth, **strategic hiring**, and **expansion into adjacent services** (networking, security, energy). Consolidation/acquisition is now a major growth path at the top.
- **The numbers (2026 CE Pro 100):** ~$8.2B combined revenue; **median firm ~$4.7M revenue, ~28 employees, 23 years in business**, ~$292k revenue per employee. The point for Ray: these aren't unicorns — they're disciplined small businesses that compounded. The *median* is a 28-person shop, and they all started where Jamieson is.
- **Watch the competition on the monitoring angle:** productized remote-monitoring services already exist (e.g. **Snowbird Home Monitor** — cellular water/temp/humidity/power monitoring, subscriptions from ~$9.99/mo with a free device). Jamieson's edge isn't the sensor — it's being the *local pro who installs, integrates with Starlink, AND physically responds.* Don't compete on the gadget; compete on the local install-and-respond bundle.
- **Unit economics that match Jamieson's model:** established smart-home install businesses run **15–25% margins** (mirrors our locked hardware markup), owner income commonly **$58k–$215k/yr**, and the consistent finding is that **recurring monthly monitoring fees are what boost profitability** above project markup alone. One financial model shows EBITDA scaling from ~$193k toward multi-million by year 5 *driven by package attach + recurring contracts* — again, the managed plan.
