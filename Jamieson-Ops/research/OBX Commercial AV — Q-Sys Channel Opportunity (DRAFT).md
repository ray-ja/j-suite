# OBX Commercial AV / Q-Sys — Channel Opportunity Brief

*Research brief on opening a high-margin commercial AV channel for Jamieson Automation, built on Ray's deepest technical edge: PLC/automation and Q-Sys audio/control experience that almost no local competitor can match. Targets OBX restaurants, bars, and venues. Draft — directional; verify each venue's current owner/contact and confirm live pricing before pitching.*

**Why this channel:** Ray's Q-Sys and advanced-automation skillset is rare and expensive on the open market — it's the highest-skill, highest-margin work he can do, and it's *not* time-competing with residential rental-tech installs (commercial AV is project-based, scoped, and scheduled). One venue install can equal many residential doors in revenue, and commercial clients pay for reliability and a pro who actually understands networked audio. This is the channel that best monetizes the scarce-skilled-time constraint: fewer jobs, much higher ticket.

---

## 1 · The opportunity in one paragraph

OBX hospitality runs on sound and atmosphere — multi-room restaurants, bars with live music, waterfront decks, and event spaces that need zoned audio, paging, and simple control. Most of these venues are running consumer or patched-together gear that fails, can't zone properly, or needs a "guy" every time something breaks. A networked **Q-Sys** (or QSC commercial) platform does what consumer gear can't: independent volume per zone, scheduled scenes, paging over music, AV switching for game days, and remote management/monitoring. Ray can design, install, program, **and** support these systems — the program-and-support piece is exactly where most low-voltage installers fall short, and it's where the margin and the recurring relationship live.

## 3 · What to sell

Package it as tiers, like the rental-tech model, so it's easy to quote and scope:

**A. Multi-zone audio (the core).**
Independent volume and source per area — dining room, bar, patio/deck, restrooms, kitchen. Background music that's actually controllable, not one volume knob for the whole building. This is the #1 pain and the anchor sale.

**B. Paging & announcements.**
Page over music (last call, waitlist, staff pages, emergency announcements) with automatic music ducking — a Q-Sys core strength and a clear operational value to managers.

**C. AV switching & video.**
TV/source management for sports bars (route any game to any screen), projector/screen for events, simple "scene" presets staff can actually use.

**D. Control & scheduling (the differentiator).**
A simple wall panel or tablet interface: "open," "happy hour," "live music," "close" scenes that set zones, sources, and levels in one tap. Scheduled changes (patio off at 10pm). This is the programming layer competitors skip — and where Ray's automation background shines.

**E. The network underneath.**
Q-Sys is networked AV — it needs proper, isolated, business-grade networking (Ray's Ubiquiti specialty). Most failures of "smart" venue audio are really network failures; owning the network is owning the reliability.

**F. Recurring support/management (the real prize).**
Remote monitoring, firmware, scene tweaks, priority service. Same managed-plan logic as rental-tech — recurring revenue on top of the project, and the reason the relationship compounds.

## 4 · Pricing posture

Commercial AV is **value-and-complexity priced**, not hourly — and the numbers are far above residential. Use these directional bands (verify against current supplier quotes before committing):

| Venue scope | Typical installed range |
|---|---|
| Small / single-zone bar | ~$2,000–5,000 |
| Mid-size restaurant, 2–4 zones | ~$5,000–15,000 |
| Large multi-zone + paging + AV | ~$15,000–50,000+ |

Per-zone runs roughly **$1,500–4,000** depending on speaker density, DSP, and control. (Sources below.)

**Posture for Jamieson:**

- **Hardware + design/programming/labor as separate lines**, same transparent model as rental-tech — but here the **programming and commissioning is the premium line**, not an afterthought. That's the skilled work and where margin concentrates.
- **Lead with a paid design/assessment** for larger jobs (site walk + zone plan + proposal). It qualifies the buyer and gets paid for the expertise.
- **Tiered packages** (e.g. "Core Zoned Audio," "Bar + Sports AV," "Full Venue Control") to make quoting fast and let the venue pick a comfort level — mirrors the per-door tier playbook.
- **Attach a support/management plan** to every install; price it monthly. This is the recurring engine.
- **Margin reality:** commercial AV commonly runs higher project margins than residential because the programming/integration labor (Ray's edge) can't be commoditized. Don't underprice the skill — the buyer is paying for "it works and stays working during a packed Saturday night."

## 5 · The local-competition gap (Jamieson's wedge)

**The one verified incumbent: Soundwaves Integrated Solutions** (sndwaves.com) — the established OBX custom-electronics contractor, in business since 2004, explicitly doing multi-zone amplification and commercial audio for restaurants, hotels, offices, and rentals. They are the real competitor to beat — well-entrenched, broad, and local. Other NC AV integrators that surfaced (CO-DA, Carolina Custom Sound, AVA, etc.) are **Charlotte-area, ~5+ hours away** — not local competition, and a sign the OBX has thin specialized-AV coverage. Treat any *other* "OBX AV" names with caution; several search hits looked auto-generated. Bottom line: it's likely a near-monopoly (Soundwaves) plus distant out-of-area firms — a soft field for a second credible local specialist.

The structural gap that is Ray's wedge — even against an entrenched generalist like Soundwaves:

1. **Programming depth is rare — even Soundwaves leads with "audio," not control programming.** Plenty of outfits can hang speakers and pull cable. Very few local installers can truly **program and commission a Q-Sys/DSP control system** — scenes, paging logic, scheduling, network design. That's specialized work Ray already does at a high level. Compete on *control depth*, not on being another speaker installer. This is the moat.
2. **Reliability + responsiveness gap.** Hospitality's complaint about AV vendors is almost always "it breaks and no one comes." A *local* pro who installs, programs, **and** answers the phone — backed by remote monitoring — directly attacks that pain.
3. **The network blind spot.** Networked AV fails when the network is an afterthought. Ray's Ubiquiti/networking specialty means he builds the foundation others bolt on badly.
4. **Proof in hand.** The escape-room automation work (OBX-Scape Rooms) is a genuine, local, demonstrable Q-Sys/control reference — most competitors can't show comparable control-programming chops.

**Wedge statement:** *"The only OBX shop that designs the network, installs the audio, AND programs the control so it actually runs itself on a busy night — and is local enough to fix it fast."*
