# PM Portfolio & Volume-Deal Playbook

*How to structure, price, and pitch a multi-door deal to a property manager. This is the channel that scales Jamieson — one PM relationship delivers many doors, which beats chasing single owners one at a time. Draft — internal. Pair with the PM Pitch & Objection Guide, the Proposal template, and the Volume-Deal Calculator (this folder).*

**Why this is the priority:** the scaled-operator research is unambiguous — the firms that grew past solo did it by landing a **volume channel** (a builder, a PM book, a referral network), not by selling harder to individuals. Jamieson already has the product, the packages, and the recurring plan. The missing lever is the channel. A PM is that channel.

---

## 1 · The core idea — 1 PM = many doors

Selling to individual owners is linear: every door is a fresh pitch, site visit, proposal, follow-up, and a separate trip. Ray's scarce skilled time is the bottleneck, so linear selling caps the business at how many sales cycles he can personally run.

A property manager flips that. **One relationship, one pitch, one portfolio proposal → many doors**, scheduled in batches. The PM also becomes *renewable*: every new property they take on next season comes to you at near-zero acquisition cost.

**The time math (illustrative, on an 8-door book):**

| | Chase 8 single owners | Land 1 PM (8 doors) |
|---|---|---|
| Sales cycles | 8 separate | 1 |
| Ray's selling/coordination time | ~40 hrs (~5 hr/owner) | ~10 hrs |
| Mobilizations / trips | 8 separate | batched |
| Next year's new doors | start from zero again | flow in through the PM |

Same 8 doors, **~75% less of Ray's scarce time on acquisition** — and a channel that keeps giving. That is the whole game given Ray's constraint.

---

## 2 · The consolidated-vendor value prop (what the PM gets)

This is what you sell the PM — not gadgets, *operational relief*:

- **One vendor, one standard.** Every door built and documented identically. No more juggling "a WiFi guy, a lock guy, a camera guy."
- **One number to call.** A guest issue at any property routes to one partner who already knows the setup.
- **One invoice, predictable budgeting.** Per-door pricing the PM can forecast and pass to owners cleanly.
- **Volume pricing.** Committing the book earns a per-door discount they can't get one-off (see §3).
- **Priority scheduling.** Portfolio clients go to the front of the install/repair queue.
- **A portfolio they can manage.** Standardized codes, cameras, and a managed plan that keeps the whole book hands-off — fewer 2 a.m. calls to *their* team.
- **Owner-ready proposals.** You hand them itemized, signable proposals they can put in front of owners (you'll even run the owner conversation).

The PM's win: easier properties, happier guests, better reviews, and they look like the hero who upgraded the owners' assets at no cost or risk to themselves.

## 3 · Volume-deal pricing structure

Start from the standard per-door list price (hardware at cost + 15–25% markup, separate from flat labor — unchanged). Then apply a **committed-volume break** off the installed per-door price:

| Committed doors | Volume break (off per-door installed) | Managed plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | List price | $75 / mo / home |
| 3–5 | **5% off** | $75 / mo / home |
| 6–10 | **8% off** | $65 / mo / home |
| 11+ | **12% off** | $55 / mo / home |

**Per-door list anchors:** Essentials **$1,300** · Plus **$3,534** · Pro **$6,202**.

**Where the discount comes from — and why margin survives:** the break is funded by **batching efficiency**, not by cutting the build. Ten doors in one portfolio means one mobilization, repeated setup rhythm, and amortized travel — your *cost to deliver each door drops*, so a per-door discount can be real while total contribution still rises. Hardware is still billed at cost + markup (margin visible on every line); you are discounting the price, knowing your delivery cost per door is lower at volume. **Never discount below the point where hardware margin + batched labor still clears your floor** — the calculator flags this.

**Rules of the road:**
- The break requires a **committed door count** (a portfolio agreement), not "maybe." That commitment is what you're paying for.
- Lead with the managed plan — recurring revenue is the real prize, and volume makes it bigger and stickier.
- Offer a **single test door at list** to skeptics, with the volume break unlocking when they commit the book.

## 4 · Sample portfolio proposal (worked example)

**Client:** "Outer Banks Premier Rentals" — a PM with **8 vacation rentals**.
**Recommended mix:** 3 Essentials, 4 Plus, 1 Pro. **Door count 8 → 8% volume break · managed plan $65/mo.**

| Package | List / door | Deal / door (−8%) | Qty | Line total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Essentials | $1,300 | $1,196.00 | 3 | $3,588.00 |
| Plus | $3,534 | $3,251.28 | 4 | $13,005.12 |
| Pro | $6,202 | $5,705.84 | 1 | $5,705.84 |
| **Install total** | | | **8** | **$22,298.96** |

- **List total would be $24,238 → PM saves $1,939** by committing the book.
- **Recurring:** 8 homes × $65/mo = **$520/mo = $6,240/yr**, and it renews.
- **First-year value of the relationship:** ~$22,300 install + $6,240 recurring = **~$28,500** — from *one* sale, plus every new property they add next season.

**How to present it:** use the standard Proposal template, add a "Portfolio Pricing" line showing list vs. volume price (so the saving is visible), and attach the per-door scope. One signature covers the book; schedule the installs in batched waves.

## 5 · How to land it (the play)

1. **Target the right PMs.** Mid-size books (5–20 doors) are the sweet spot — big enough to matter, small enough to decide fast. OBX is dense with them.
2. **Open with relief, not tech.** Use the 30-second hook from the Pitch Guide: their 2 a.m. calls and bad reviews trace to the same few things; you fix all of them to one standard, per door.
3. **Offer the test door.** Lowest-friction yes: "Give me your most problem-prone property at list; if you're happy, we roll out the book at volume pricing."
4. **Bring the portfolio proposal.** Show list-vs-volume side by side and the managed-plan recurring line. Make the commitment the thing that unlocks the savings.
5. **Run the owner conversation for them.** Hand them owner-ready, signable proposals; offer to talk to owners directly. Remove their work.
6. **Lock the managed plan.** That's the renewable revenue and the reason the relationship compounds.

## 6 · Why this scales *both* businesses

The same PM relationship is a channel for **OBX Lot Solutions** too — the PM who trusts Jamieson for tech is the natural buyer for recurring property cleanup, pressure washing, and home-watch-adjacent services on the same book. Land the PM once; sell across both companies. One channel, two revenue streams, shared scheduling and trust.
