Walking into a property manager's office

You're not selling gadgets — you're selling fewer headaches and better reviews.
The 30-second hook (say this first)

“Hi, I'm Ray with Jamieson Automation — I do the tech side of rentals here on the Outer Banks. Most of your 2 a.m. calls and bad reviews come down to the same handful of things: WiFi dropping, a lock acting up, or internet going out in a storm. I fix all of that to one standard, per door, and then keep it running — so your team stops chasing it. Can I show you what that looks like?”

The per-door framing

  • One price, per door, installed. Every property gets the same reliable build — no guessing.
  • Three tiers so owners pick their comfort level: Essentials $1,300 · Plus $3,534 · Pro $6,202.
  • Itemized & honest. Hardware at cost plus markup, install labor separate — they see exactly what they pay for.
  • Then I keep it running — managed plan $49–99/mo: monitoring, updates, guest codes, storm checks.

What's in a door

  • Keypad lock — per-guest codes that expire at checkout. No more lockouts.
  • Whole-home WiFi built for streaming guests, not a box-store router.
  • Starlink backup — internet stays up in a storm (Plus/Pro).
  • Exterior cameras — entries & driveway, privacy-safe.
  • One vendor, one number for the whole portfolio.
Top 5 objections — and what to say
1. “We already have a guy for that.”
“That's great — most PMs do, for one piece of it. I'm the one vendor who does all of it to the same standard, every door, and documents it so anyone on your team can manage codes and pull up a camera. If your guy's solid, keep him — but next time something breaks at 9 p.m., try me once and compare.” Pivot: offer to do one property as a test.
2. “That's too expensive.”
“Compared to what, though? One bad review from dead WiFi can cost a peak-week booking — that's more than the whole install. This is a one-time per-door cost that prevents the calls and refunds that eat your season. And Essentials starts at $1,300 if you want to start small.” Pivot: anchor against the cost of a refund / lost week, not against a cheaper router.
3. “The owners won't pay for it.”
“Totally fair — so let's make it easy for them. It's an itemized proposal they sign directly, it raises their nightly rate and review scores, and it protects their asset. You don't front anything — I handle the owner conversation if you want. You just get easier properties to manage.” Pivot: position PM as the hero who brought the owner an upgrade, at no cost/risk to the PM.
4. “We don't want cameras — guest privacy.”
“Agreed, and that's exactly how I do it — exterior only, entries and driveway, never inside, and always disclosed to guests. They're there to protect the property and settle disputes, not watch anyone. And cameras are optional — the lock and WiFi alone solve most of your headaches.” Pivot: drop cameras, keep the lock + WiFi core.
5. “We're slammed right now — not a good time.”
“That's the best reason to do it — slammed means every dropped WiFi is a call your team can't take. I do the work; you don't lift a finger. Let's pick one property in the next two weeks, get it dialed in, and you decide from there. No big commitment.” Pivot: shrink the ask to a single property and a near-term date.

The close / the ask

Always leave with a next step, not a “think about it.” Pick one:

“Give me one property — your most problem-prone door — and let me prove it. If you're happy, we roll it out across the portfolio.”

Or: “Let me put a number on it. Tell me how many doors and I'll have an itemized proposal to you tomorrow — no obligation.”

Remember

  • Lead with their pain, not your gear
  • Talk doors & reviews, not specs
  • Shrink every “no” to one test door
  • Name the next step out loud
  • Leave the one-pager behind
