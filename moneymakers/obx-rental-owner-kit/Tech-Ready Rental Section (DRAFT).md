## Why your rental's tech makes or breaks your reviews

Guests don't review your house — they review their *experience* of it. And on the Outer Banks, the experience runs on four things that are invisible when they work and catastrophic when they don't: **getting in the door, the WiFi, the house not flooding or freezing while it's empty, and feeling safe.** Nail these four and you've removed the top causes of 1-star reviews, refund demands, and 2 a.m. phone calls. This section walks each one, tells you what "guest-ready" looks like, and flags where doing it cheap will cost you later.

Score yourself: **✅ done right · ⚠️ partial / risky · ❌ missing.** Tally at the end.

## 1 · Keyless Entry — the #1 friction point

A lockbox with a shared code is the most common rental tech, and the worst. Codes get passed around, keys get lost, and a late check-in with a jammed lockbox is an instant bad review.

**Guest-ready looks like:**

- [ ] A **keypad smart lock** on the main entry (no physical key handoff)
- [ ] A **unique code per guest** that activates at check-in and **auto-expires at checkout**
- [ ] Codes generated automatically from your booking platform (Airbnb/Vrbo) or PMS — not typed in by hand each time
- [ ] A backup physical key stored securely off-site for emergencies
- [ ] Lock batteries checked / low-battery alerts enabled (a dead lock at check-in is a disaster)

**Why it matters:** keyless entry eliminates lockouts, the single most common check-in complaint, and per-guest codes mean no one keeps working access after their stay. It also lets cleaners and contractors in on their own schedule without you driving over.

## 2 · Guest WiFi — the amenity guests check first

Slow or unreliable WiFi is one of the most damaging things a coastal rental can have — remote workers and streaming families notice immediately, and they say so in reviews.

**Guest-ready looks like:**

- [ ] **Whole-home coverage** — strong signal in every bedroom, the deck, and the kitchen (not just by the router)
- [ ] A **dedicated guest network**, separate from any owner/smart-home devices
- [ ] The network name and password posted clearly (and in your welcome book)
- [ ] **Business-grade equipment** (mesh/access points), not the single box the ISP dropped off
- [ ] A way to **restart/​monitor it remotely** so a drop doesn't wait for the next guest to report it

**Why it matters:** "the WiFi everywhere" is now a baseline expectation, not a luxury. One dead spot in the primary bedroom can cost you a five-star review. A separate guest network also keeps your locks, cameras, and thermostats off the network guests can see.

## 3 · Leak & Temperature Sensors — protect the asset while it's empty

This is the one owners skip and regret. Your house sits empty between guests and all winter. A supply line behind the fridge, a failed water heater, or a dead HVAC in a cold snap can run for days before anyone knows — turning a cheap part into a five-figure claim.

**Guest-ready (really, owner-ready) looks like:**

- [ ] **Water-leak sensors** at every wet point — under sinks, behind the fridge/washer, at the water heater
- [ ] An **automatic water shut-off valve** on the main that kills the water the instant a leak is detected
- [ ] **Temperature & humidity sensors** that alert you if the house drifts toward freezing or into mold territory
- [ ] Alerts that reach **you and a local responder** (not just a chime in an empty house)
- [ ] *Bonus:* ask your insurer — many discount premiums for monitored leak/shut-off systems

**Why it matters:** water damage is the most frequent and most expensive thing that happens to an unoccupied home. Sensors plus an auto-shutoff are the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy, and they protect the asset, not just the guest experience.

> **This is what keeps a small problem from becoming a destroyed season.** Jamieson installs leak/temp sensors and auto-shutoff, and (managed plan) monitors them so someone actually responds. → *[Rental-Ready Tech package]*

## 4 · Security Cameras — protection done right (and legally)

Cameras protect your property and settle disputes — but done wrong they violate guest privacy and platform rules, which is its own disaster.

**Guest-ready looks like:**

- [ ] Cameras at **exterior points only** — entries and driveway
- [ ] **Never** any camera (or listening device) in interior or private spaces — this violates Airbnb/Vrbo policy and the law
- [ ] All cameras **clearly disclosed** in your listing and house rules
- [ ] Footage you can **view remotely** and pull clips from when needed
- [ ] Mounted and powered reliably (wired/PoE), not a battery cam that dies mid-season

**Why it matters:** exterior cameras deter problems, document storm damage, confirm check-out, and resolve "the neighbor complained about a party" disputes — while keeping you on the right side of privacy law and platform policy.

> **Get the coverage without the liability.** Jamieson installs exterior-only cameras, set up for remote viewing and disclosed correctly. → *[Rental-Ready Tech package]*

## 5 · The piece everyone forgets — keep it online in a storm

Every system above depends on the internet. On the Outer Banks, the power and the cable go out *exactly* when you need monitoring most — during a storm. If your leak sensor can't phone home because the cable's down, it's not protecting anything.

**Guest-ready looks like:**

- [ ] **Backup internet (Starlink)** that keeps the home online and reporting during outages
- [ ] Smart-home hub/network on backup power where it counts
- [ ] You still get alerts when the grid is down
