# The OBX Pricing & Seasonality Cheat Sheet

*Part of the OBX Rental Owner's Operations Kit. How Outer Banks vacation-rental demand actually moves through the year, and a simple framework to price for it. This is a planning tool, not financial advice — your numbers depend on your home, location, and amenities. Use it to build a pricing rhythm you can adjust from real booking data.*

> **The core idea:** OBX rental demand is dramatically seasonal. A huge share of annual revenue is earned in a short summer window, so smart pricing means charging what summer will bear, filling the shoulders aggressively, and not leaving the off-season empty when a little discount would book it.

---

## The OBX demand curve (general pattern)

| Season | Months | Demand | Pricing posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Peak summer** | mid-June – mid-August | Highest — books earliest, Sat–Sat weeks | Top rates; **7-night Sat–Sat** minimums; raise as you fill |
| **Shoulder (early)** | late April – early June | Strong, growing | Strong rates; flexible minimums; capture early birds + events |
| **Shoulder (late)** | late August – October | Strong (warm water, fewer crowds) | Near-peak early Sept; ease down through Oct; promote "secret season" |
| **Off-season** | November – March | Low | Discount to fill; flexible/short stays; monthly/snowbird angle; holidays spike |

**Key dynamics to price around:**
- ★ **Summer is Saturday-to-Saturday week territory** — most OBX homes rent full weeks in peak, not nights.
- ★ **Late summer/early fall is underrated** — water's warm, crowds thin, families with no school-age kids and couples book it. Don't crater your rate Sept 1.
- ★ **Booking lead time is long for summer** — peak weeks book months ahead (winter/spring). Have next summer's calendar and rates open early.
- ★ **Events move the needle** — fishing tournaments, festivals, marathons, holidays. Keep a local-events list and price up around them.
- ★ **Off-season isn't hopeless** — remote workers, snowbirds, holiday gatherings, and shorter getaways will book a well-priced winter home; an empty home earns nothing.

---

## A simple pricing framework (4 steps)

**1. Set your peak-week anchor.** Research comparable OBX homes (same area, bedrooms, amenities — pool/hot tub/oceanfront move price a lot) for a peak-July week. That's your top rate.

**2. Build the seasonal ladder off the anchor.** A rough starting scaffold to then tune with data:
- Peak summer: 100% (anchor)
- Early/late shoulder: ~70–85%
- Off-season: ~40–55%
- Holidays (off-season spikes): bump toward shoulder pricing
*(These are starting ratios to adjust — your real numbers come from how fast you fill.)*

**3. Adjust with live signals.** Filling fast for a week 4 months out? You underpriced — raise it. Two weeks out and empty? Drop it or open shorter stays. Let the calendar tell you.

**4. Account for your costs per booking.** Cleaning/turnover, supplies, management/listing fees, maintenance reserve, taxes (★ NC + county occupancy taxes apply — confirm current rates with the county). Know your **net** per booking, not just the headline nightly rate.

---

## Minimum-stay strategy
- **Peak summer:** full-week, Sat–Sat (matches OBX turnover rhythm and demand).
- **Shoulders:** shorter minimums (3–4 nights) to capture long weekends.
- **Off-season:** flexible / short stays, even 2-night, to fill otherwise-dead dates; consider weekly/monthly for snowbirds.

## Fees & the guest-math reality
- Be transparent — surprise fees tank reviews and conversion.
- Cleaning fee should ~cover your actual turnover cost (a coastal turn runs higher; see Turnover Checklist).
- ★ Factor occupancy taxes and platform fees into how your total price reads to a guest vs. competitors.

## Quick "am I pricing right?" gut checks
- Booked solid months ahead? You're likely **underpriced** for peak.
- Lots of empty shoulder/off-season dates? You're likely **overpriced** for those, or need shorter minimums.
- Great reviews but soft bookings? Probably **price/visibility**, not the product.
- Bad reviews dragging bookings? Fix the **operations** first (the rest of this kit) — price can't outrun a 3-star average.
