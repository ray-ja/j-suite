# The OBX Storm-Prep Plan

*Part of the OBX Rental Owner's Operations Kit. A before-during-after hurricane and storm plan for an Outer Banks rental — the barrier-island reality that hurricane season (June 1–Nov 30) is part of your operating calendar, not a rare event. Decide who does what now, while it's calm.*

> **The one rule:** make the plan *before* you need it. The owners who handle storms well decided in May who secures the house, where the supplies are, and how guests get notified. Fill in the [brackets] and keep this where your team can find it.

## BEFORE THE SEASON (do once, in May)
- [ ] Confirm insurance is current; understand wind/flood coverage and your deductible
- [ ] ★ Photograph the entire property inside + out (dated) for claims baseline
- [ ] Stock storm supplies (list below) and know where they're stored
- [ ] Confirm who secures the home if a storm comes while it's rented or vacant
- [ ] ★ Check shutters/coverings, straps, and that loose items have a storage spot
- [ ] Sign up for county emergency alerts
- [ ] Draft your guest storm-communication message (template below)
- [ ] Know your evacuation reality: ★ OBX visitor/resident evacuations are ordered separately — guests usually must leave first

## WHEN A STORM IS FORECAST (48–72 hours out)
- [ ] Track the system; watch for county evacuation orders
- [ ] ★ Contact current/upcoming guests — send the storm message; coordinate departure or cancellation per your policy
- [ ] Secure or store: outdoor furniture, grills, umbrellas, beach gear, décor, trash bins
- [ ] Install/confirm shutters or window coverings
- [ ] ★ Shut off water at the main if the home will be empty (burst line in an outage is a top claim)
- [ ] Charge devices; check battery backups, sump pump, flashlights
- [ ] Set fridge/freezer cold; consider what's perishable
- [ ] Confirm your local contact is ready to act
- [ ] ★ Final photos of secured condition

## DURING / IMMEDIATELY AFTER
- [ ] Do not enter until it's safe and access is reopened (bridges/roads close)
- [ ] Monitor remote sensors/cameras if installed (power-loss, leak, temp)
- [ ] Coordinate your local contact for first safe inspection

## AFTER THE STORM
- [ ] ★ Get eyes on the property fast — early damage is cheaper and better for the claim
- [ ] Document everything (photo/video) **before** any cleanup
- [ ] Check: roof, windows, water intrusion, flooding, power, HVAC restart
- [ ] Clear debris before it causes drainage/pest issues
- [ ] File insurance claim promptly with your documentation
- [ ] ★ Assess re-rentability; update calendar and notify affected guests
- [ ] Restock any used storm supplies

## Storm supply kit (keep stocked Jun–Nov)
Flashlights + batteries · battery/crank radio · first-aid kit · bottled water · basic tools · tarps + rope · duct tape · trash bags · phone power banks · manual can opener · shutoff/water-main tool · printed copy of this plan + contacts.

*Tip: know your cancellation/refund stance for mandatory evacuations BEFORE the season — and consider whether to require/recommend travel insurance in your listing.*

---

## ★ Smart monitoring makes this far easier
If your home is hours away, sensors change the game during a storm: a water-leak sensor, a temperature monitor, a power-loss alert, and a camera let you watch your home through the event from anywhere — *if* the internet stays up (backup internet like Starlink keeps it reporting when cable fails). Monitoring tells you something's wrong; a local contact fixes it. The two together are the real storm plan.
