Closing up for the off-season

The Outer Banks doesn't get brutal winters, but it gets enough freezes, humidity, and quiet months to wreck an unwatched home. Here's how to close up right.

Freeze & plumbing protection

A hard freeze on a coastal home with water in the lines is how owners come back to a flooded house. Even a mild OBX winter throws a few nights below freezing.

  • Shut off the main and, for longer closures, drain the lines (or have them blown out).
  • Keep HVAC at a minimum hold (e.g., ~55°F) if you don't fully winterize, so pipes don't freeze.
  • Insulate exposed pipes, hose bibs, and pipes in the crawlspace.
  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses and outdoor showers (very common on OBX homes).

Humidity & mold — the silent off-season killer

Closed-up coastal homes trap moisture. Without airflow or dehumidification, you get musty air, mold on walls and HVAC, and ruined soft goods — discovered months later.

  • Run HVAC in a mode that manages humidity, or use a dehumidifier on a drain.
  • Keep target humidity around 45–55%.
  • Leave interior doors and closets cracked open for airflow.
  • Clean the HVAC drain line and pan before closing — a clogged line floods quietly.

Pests & critters

  • Remove all food, empty the pantry, clean the fridge (and prop it open if powering down).
  • Seal entry points — mice and insects find gaps in an empty, quiet house fast.
  • Take out the trash and clean drains so nothing attracts pests.

Exterior & salt

  • Clean gutters after leaf-drop so winter rain drains.
  • Rinse salt off windows, rails, and fixtures; a soft wash before closing keeps algae and salt from etching surfaces over winter.
  • Stow or cover outdoor furniture and grills.
  • Check crawlspace vents and skirting.

The winter check schedule

Closing up is step one. Step two is someone confirming it stayed closed up. A monthly walk-through catches the tripped breaker, the dripping mini-split, the mouse, or the storm-loosened shingle before spring. That's the whole case for off-season home watch.
