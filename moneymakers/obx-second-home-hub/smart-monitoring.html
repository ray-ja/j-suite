Watch the house from your phone

Sensors don't replace a real check — but they buy you something a monthly visit can't: a real-time alert the moment something goes wrong. Here's the smart-monitoring stack that makes sense for an empty OBX home.

The five layers, in priority order

Don't buy a "smart home." Buy, in this order, the layers that prevent the most expensive failures in a vacant coastal house:

1 · Water (do this first)

Water causes the most empty-home damage, so it gets top priority. A few Wi-Fi leak sensors ($15–25 each) at the water heater, under sinks, behind toilets, and in the HVAC drain pan will ping your phone the instant they get wet. Step up to an automatic water-shutoff valve that kills the supply on a leak or freeze — the closest thing to insurance against a burst line.

2 · Climate (freeze & humidity)

A smart thermostat lets you hold temperature and get alerts if the house drifts toward a freeze. Add a temperature/humidity sensor to catch a dead HVAC or rising dampness before mold starts (target 45–55% RH). Critical for the off-season.

3 · Access & smart locks

A smart lock with keypad codes lets cleaners, contractors, and your home-watch service in with codes you control and revoke from your phone — no hidden key, no lockbox — and logs every entry. For a part-time rental, this is also how you automate guest access.

4 · Cameras

A doorbell cam and a couple of exterior cameras give you eyes on the property and a deterrent. For a vacant home, wired PoE cameras to a local recorder are more reliable than battery cams (no dead batteries mid-storm); battery/Wi-Fi cams are easier to DIY. Either way, position them on entries and the most weather-exposed elevations.

5 · Connectivity — the part people forget

Every sensor above is useless if the internet drops. Many OBX homes have spotty or seasonal service. Options:

  • Keep internet on year-round (don't cancel in the off-season) and put the router on a smart plug so you can remotely power-cycle it.
  • Add a cellular backup for critical alerts, or
  • Use Starlink where cable/DSL is unreliable — increasingly the go-to for remote stretches of the Banks, and it keeps cameras and sensors online through outages.
  • Make sure Wi-Fi actually reaches the crawlspace, far rooms, and exterior cameras — a mesh node or access point often matters more than the sensors themselves.

DIY vs. professionally installed

DIY it

Leak sensors, a smart thermostat, a video doorbell, and a couple of Wi-Fi cameras are genuinely DIY-friendly. Start here — see our recommended gear.

Have it installed

Whole-home water shutoff, PoE camera systems, mesh networks, and Starlink are worth professional install — clean cabling, reliable coverage, and one system that works together. That's a conversation we can have.
