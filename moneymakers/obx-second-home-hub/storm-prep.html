Outer Banks storm prep for second homes

The handful of steps that stop the storm damage owners actually pay for — most of which you can do (or have done) before you ever leave the island.

On the Outer Banks, "storm prep" doesn't only mean hurricanes. Nor'easters, summer thunderstorms, and ordinary 40-mph gusts off the water do damage all year — and when your home sits empty, a small problem has weeks to become a big one. The goal here is simple: keep water and wind out, and remove anything that can become a projectile.

Why coastal storm prep is different

Three forces make OBX homes uniquely vulnerable: wind-driven rain that finds every gap, salt that corrodes fasteners and seals over time, and storm surge / standing water near grade. Prep that works inland (a few sandbags, taping windows) misses the point here. What matters most is the building envelope — roof, windows, doors, and drainage — plus killing the interior water sources before you go.

Exterior: the 9 high-impact steps

  • Clear gutters & downspouts. Clogged gutters overflow into soffits and walls — the most common "invisible" storm damage on the Banks.
  • Secure or stow loose items. Deck furniture, grills, planters, trash cans, doormats, flags — anything not bolted down becomes a projectile or ends up in a neighbor's window.
  • Check the roof & flashing. Lifted shingles, loose ridge caps, and failed flashing are where wind gets under and peels. Fix before season, not during.
  • Inspect & seal windows and doors. Re-caulk gaps, confirm weatherstripping, and make sure storm shutters (if any) actually close and latch.
  • Trim back trees and limbs near the roof, deck, and power lines.
  • Confirm drainage away from the foundation and clear any French drains or scuppers.
  • Photograph the property — every elevation and the roof — so you have dated "before" images for any insurance claim.
  • Check the crawlspace / pilings for standing water, vents, and any loose skirting.
  • Test the sump pump (and its battery backup) if you have one.

Interior: stop water from inside

Half of "storm" damage is actually plumbing and HVAC that fails while you're gone. Before a storm — or any long absence:

  • Shut off the main water supply if no one will be there. A burst supply line does more damage than most storms.
  • Set HVAC to a safe humidity/temperature hold so the home doesn't swing to mold-growing dampness.
  • Unplug sensitive electronics and consider whole-home surge protection — lightning is common here.
  • Move valuables and documents off the ground floor.
  • Place leak sensors at the water heater, under sinks, behind toilets, and in the HVAC drain-pan.
The one that costs the most: a supply line or water-heater leak in an empty house. By the time it's discovered weeks later, you're looking at flooring, drywall, and mold remediation. Shutting the main and dropping in $20 leak sensors is the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy.

If the home will be empty during the season

Everything above assumes someone closes the loop. If you're out of state, the missing piece is a local check before and after each significant storm — to close shutters, confirm the prep held, and document any damage immediately (insurers reward fast, photo-backed claims). FAQ

When should I do storm prep on the Outer Banks?
Do the exterior envelope work (roof, gutters, sealing, trimming) before hurricane season starts on June 1, and keep the home "storm-ready" through November. Loose-item and interior steps happen before any specific storm or extended absence.
Should I tape or board my windows?
Tape does almost nothing. Properly rated storm shutters or pre-cut plywood panels protect against flying debris; if your home doesn't have them, prioritizing impact protection on the largest/most exposed openings is the higher-value move.
