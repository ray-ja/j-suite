Should you hire a home-watch service?

DIY, lean on a neighbor, or pay a pro? Here's the honest comparison for an empty Outer Banks home — including the parts people don't think about until a claim.

First, what home watch is (and isn't)

Home watch is a scheduled visual inspection of a home you don't live in — inside and out — with a photo report every visit. The watcher looks for leaks, humidity, pests, storm and break-in damage, runs the water, and confirms systems are behaving. It is not property management: no rentals, no tenants, no booking calendars. Just confirmation your home is safe, dry, and secure between your visits.

The three options, honestly

🙋

Do it yourself

Cost: travel + your time.
Reality: only works if you're close enough to drive over on short notice — which, for most second-home owners, is the whole problem. Storms and leaks don't wait for your next trip.

👋

A neighbor or friend

Cost: "free" (a favor).
Reality: inconsistent, untrained, uninsured, and hard to hold accountable. They won't spot a failing mini-split or document a claim — and you'll feel bad asking every storm.

✅

A professional service

Cost: from ~$55/visit.
Reality: scheduled, insured, trained on coastal failure points, and documented with photos — which protects both your home and your insurance position.

What tips owners toward a pro

  • You're out of state (or far enough that "just drive over" isn't real).
  • Your insurer expects periodic checks on a vacant home — and you want the photo paper trail. (See vacancy & insurance.)
  • The home is high-value or rented part-time, so problems are expensive and turnovers matter.
  • You want to stop thinking about it — the actual product here is peace of mind.
💡 Best of both: pair a pro's monthly visits with smart sensors. Sensors catch problems in real time; the visit confirms, documents, and fixes. Together they cover the gaps each leaves alone.

What a good OBX home-watch visit includes

  • Exterior walk: roof/shingles (from ground), gutters, siding, windows, deck, crawlspace/pilings.
  • Interior walk: every room, ceilings/walls for stains, under sinks, around water heater & HVAC.
  • Run faucets, check toilets, confirm no leaks or smells.
  • Verify HVAC/humidity, breakers, and that nothing's tripped.
  • Pre- and post-storm checks as needed.
  • A dated photo report you keep on file.

FAQ

How often should I have my OBX home checked?
Monthly is the common baseline for a seasonal home, with extra pre-/post-storm visits in hurricane season. High-value or rented homes often go bi-weekly or weekly.
Is home watch the same as a property manager?
No. Property managers handle rentals, guests, and bookings. Home watch simply inspects and documents a vacant home. Many owners use both for a part-time rental.
