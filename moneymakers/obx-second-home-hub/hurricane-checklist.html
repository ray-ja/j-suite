OBX hurricane checklist for absent owners

When a named storm points at the Banks and you're not here, work this list top to bottom. Print it, or send it to whoever's covering your home.

📄 Print this page (or save as PDF) and keep it with your home file. The steps are ordered — a week out, 48 hours out, after the storm.

🗓️ One week out

  • Confirm the home is storm-ready (see the storm-prep guide): roof, gutters, sealing, trees.
  • Line up a local check — a neighbor or a home-watch service that can physically prep and inspect.
  • Confirm insurance is active and you know your flood vs. wind coverage and deductibles.
  • Locate and test storm shutters / plywood panels; replace missing hardware now.
  • Charge or replace batteries in leak sensors, cameras, and sump backup.
  • Photograph every elevation + the roof and interior — dated "before" evidence.

⏱️ 48 hours out

  • Secure or stow everything loose — deck furniture, grills, planters, trash cans, doormats, flags, kayaks.
  • Close and latch shutters or install panels over the largest/most exposed windows.
  • Shut off the main water supply.
  • Set HVAC to a safe hold (or off, depending on temperature) and ensure the drain pan is clear.
  • Unplug electronics; confirm surge protection.
  • Move valuables, documents, and anything irreplaceable to an upper floor.
  • Confirm sump pump + battery backup are working.
  • Clear drains, scuppers, and downspouts one last time.
  • If you have smart monitoring, verify cameras/sensors are online and you'll get alerts.

🌀 After the storm passes

  • Get a local post-storm inspection as soon as it's safe — interior & exterior, with dated photos.
  • Mitigate water immediately — standing water and moisture become mold within 24–48 hours.
  • File insurance claims fast, with the before/after photos attached. Early, documented claims fare best.
  • Re-open the water main only after confirming no interior leaks.
  • Reset HVAC, restock, and document any losses before cleanup.
The off-island trap: the prep is only half the job — someone has to verify it held and react fast afterward. If you can't be here, that's the single most valuable thing to delegate.
