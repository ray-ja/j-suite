# Market Research & Strategy — Jamieson Automation + OBX Lot Solutions

*Prepared overnight while you slept. Deep market research across both businesses — Outer Banks–specific and US-wide — mapped to your skill set, ending in a clear call on what to push first to start making money now. Sources are linked throughout.*

---

## The one idea that ties everything together

The Outer Banks is not a normal service market. It is a market of **absentee owners and rental machines**:

- **~60% of OBX homes are second homes** — owned by people who don't live here. ([Dare County / Owners Alliance](https://outerbanksownersalliance.wildapricot.org/Second-Homes))
- **~52% of homes sit vacant** at any time (seasonal/rental), and the housing stock is dominated by weekly vacation rentals rather than long-term housing. ([Point2 / Dare County](https://www.point2homes.com/US/Neighborhood/NC/Dare-County-Demographics.html))
- **8,000–10,000+ vacation rental homes in Dare County alone** (VRBO lists 7,700+, RentByOwner 9,900+), plus thousands more in Currituck/Corolla. ([Vrbo](https://www.vrbo.com/vacation-rentals/usa/north-carolina/dare-county))
- **$2.1 billion** in visitor spending in Dare County in 2024 ($566M of it lodging); tourism is **45.5% of all local jobs**. ([Outer Banks Voice](https://www.outerbanksvoice.com/2025/08/29/dare-county-generated-2-1-billion-in-2024-visitor-spending-a-modest-drop-from-2023/))
- And there's a **real skilled-trades labor shortage** in NC — aging workforce, too few young workers replacing them. ([UNC Carolina Across 100](https://carolinaacross100.unc.edu/construction-workforce-development-addressing-labor-shortages-to-address-housing-shortages/))

Put those together and the opportunity is blunt: **thousands of expensive properties owned by people who aren't here, who need a reliable local they can trust to clean, wash, watch, fix, and tech-out their homes — in a place where reliable labor is scarce.** That is *exactly* the gap you fill. Every recommendation below is a different way to sell into that same base of property managers and second-home owners.

**Your unfair advantage:** you can do both ends of the spectrum. OBX Lot Solutions handles the cheap, recurring, brother-powered work (cash now). Jamieson handles the premium, skilled work (margin). Same customers, two price tiers, one Rolodex.

## THE DECISION — what to push first

**OBX Lot Solutions (cash now, runs on your brothers):**
1. **Pressure / soft washing** — start immediately. This is the engine.
2. **House-watch / property checks** — start immediately. The sleeper goldmine and the wedge that opens every other service.
3. Keep **junk removal** going (get the trailer).
4. Bundle in **window + gutter cleaning** on wash visits.
5. Queue **holiday lighting** to launch this fall (Q4 cash when the OBX is dead).

**Jamieson Automation (your hands, premium margin):**
1. **Starlink + vacation-rental tech packages** — push now; productized and fed by PM relationships.
2. **Commercial AV / Q-Sys** for restaurants & bars — your deepest specialty, almost no real local competition.
3. **Permanent outdoor LED lighting** — the high-ticket bet; set up this summer to sell hard in the fall.

**The wildcard worth testing:** **mobile boat fiberglass/gelcoat repair** — a rare skill you already have, in a boat-saturated coastal market, at $110/hr.

Rationale and the numbers behind each are below.

## OBX LOT SOLUTIONS — full menu, ranked

### ★ 1. Pressure & soft washing — *start now, this is the engine*
- **Why it wins:** the coast is brutal on exteriors — salt and humidity grow algae/mildew fast, so houses, roofs, decks, and driveways need washing **on a recurring (often annual) basis**. ([Walsh Roofing](https://walshroofing.com/soft-wash-roof-cleaning-cost/)) Recurring demand is the whole game.
- **Money:** the US pressure-washing industry is **$1.2B across ~32,000 businesses**, with **gross margins commonly 40–65%**. ([Fieldcamp](https://fieldcamp.ai/blog/how-much-to-charge-for-pressure-washing/), [HouseCall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/how-to-price-pressure-washing-jobs/)) Typical house exterior **$192–$400**; pricing **$0.30–$0.80/sq ft**. Roof **soft wash ~$700** (and you should soft-wash, not pressure-wash, roofs — high pressure strips shingle granules).
- **Fit:** you already own a commercial unit; the brothers can be trained; it's not time-critical like rental turnovers.
- **Move:** lead with **soft washing** (houses, roofs, siding) for the recurring annual money, plus concrete/driveway pressure washing. Sell **annual recurring plans** (everyone's salt/algae comes back).

### ★ 2. House-watch / property checks — *start now, the sleeper*
- **Why it wins:** with **60% of homes owned by absentees**, there's an enormous base of owners who legally/practically need someone checking their property. **Many insurers require periodic checks on vacant homes.** ([Personal Property Managers](https://www.personalpropertymanagers.com/absentee-home-watch.php))
- **Money:** **per-visit recurring** (weekly/bi-weekly/monthly), **near-zero equipment**, delivered with a **photo/text report**. High trust = premium and sticky. This is pure margin.
- **Why it's strategic:** it's the **wedge**. Once you're the trusted person walking the property, you're first in line to also do the washing, junk, repairs, and tech. It turns one-off jobs into a standing relationship.
- **Move:** offer tiered check plans (e.g., monthly/bi-weekly) with a branded photo report. Pitch it to the same property managers and to FSBO/owner-managed second homes.

### ● 3. Junk removal — *keep it, get the trailer*
- **Money:** **$350–$850 per job**; **solo margins 40–72%** (crew 20–35%); tipping fees ~$62/ton. ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/junk-removal/how-to-price-a-junk-removal-job/)) A solo operator can clear **$90–150k/yr**.
- **Note:** you flagged you have a truck but no trailer — that's the gating item (already on your to-do list). Good cash, low skill, pairs with clean-outs for the same absentee/PM customers.

### ● 4. Window + gutter cleaning — *bundle on wash visits*
- **Money:** windows **$150–$370/residential visit** (commercial $50–200, weekly/bi-weekly), **20–50% margin**; gutters **$0.95–$2.50/linear ft**, **35–50% margin**, needed ~twice a year. ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/window-cleaning/how-to-price-window-cleaning/), [HouseCall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/gutter-cleaning-prices/))
- **Move:** don't market these as standalone (saturated, low-ticket). **Attach** them to washing/house-watch accounts as recurring add-ons — near-zero acquisition cost.

### ● 5. Holiday lighting — *queue for Q4, big seasonal cash*
- **Why it wins:** it lands **exactly when the OBX is dead** (Nov–Dec), turning your slow season into a cash spike, and affluent second-home owners pay well for it.
- **Money:** **30%+ net margins**, materials only 10–12% of price, **$8–$35/linear ft**; a 3-person crew nets **$1,000–$1,700/day**; full-time operators clear **$100k+ per season**, with **80–90% of revenue in a 3–4 month window**. ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/holiday-lighting/how-to-start-christmas-light-business/), [Service Autopilot](https://www.serviceautopilot.com/lawn-care/how-profitable-is-the-christmas-light-business/))
- **Move:** decide by **late summer**, pre-sell in September/October. This is also the bridge to Jamieson's **permanent** lighting (below).

### ○ 6. Vacation-rental turnover cleaning — *big market, defer*
- **Reality:** cleanliness is the **#1 cause of guest complaints**, and managers struggle to staff turnover days (Fri/Sat/Sun). ([KEES](https://www.keesouterbanks.com/housekeeping)) Huge market — but **time-critical** (same-day weekend turns), which is risky with a part-time, not-fully-reliable crew. **Revisit once the crew is proven.**

## JAMIESON AUTOMATION — full menu, ranked

### ★ 1. Starlink + vacation-rental tech packages — *push now*
- **Why it wins:** **71% of travelers prefer keyless entry**; rentals need reliable guest wifi; the global vacation-rental market is **~$100B**. ([Hostfully](https://www.hostfully.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-smart-lock-integration-and-keyless-entry-for-vacation-rentals/)) Starlink pro-install demand is real enough that Starlink's own installs carry **demand surcharges of $100–$1,500** in busy areas. ([SatelliteInternet](https://www.satelliteinternet.com/providers/starlink/))
- **Fit:** you've done 3 Starlink installs already. Bundle: Starlink + mesh wifi + smart lock + thermostat + cameras = a **"rental-ready tech package"** sold per door to PMs and owners.
- **Move:** productize a flat-rate rental package; pitch it through the same property managers OBX Lot Solutions is courting. One PM = many doors.

### ★ 2. Commercial AV / Q-Sys for restaurants & bars — *your deepest edge*
- **Why it wins:** the OBX is **dense with restaurants and bars** (tourist economy), and you have **genuine Q-Sys/QSC expertise** — the exact platform used for multi-zone hospitality audio, paging, and control. ([QSC Hospitality](https://www.qsys.com/products/solutions/industry/hospitality/hospitality-solutions-restaurants/)) Very few true pros compete locally.
- **Money:** multi-zone systems are high-ticket installs **plus recurring support/programming** contracts.
- **Move:** this is relationship-sold. Walk into venues; lead with your Q-Sys background. High margin, low competition, hard for others to copy.

### ★ 3. Permanent outdoor LED lighting — *the high-ticket bet*
- **Why it wins:** systems like **Trimlight / Jellyfish / Gemstone** are **year-round** (holiday + accent + security lighting in one app-controlled install), sold to affluent homes — and the OBX is full of them. Likely **little local competition**.
- **Money:** **$1,800–$2,500+ per home** to start, **$18–$35/linear ft** installed, plus a ~$650 controller. ([JellyFish pricing](https://www.jellyfishlighting.com/pricing), [Trimlight Denver](https://trimlightdenver.com/trimlight-vs-oelo-vs-jellyfish-vs-gemstone-a-comparison-of-permanent-outdoor-lighting/)) Margins track holiday lighting (materials ~10–12%).
- **Fit:** it's technical (LED channels, controllers, app/network config, low-voltage) — squarely your wheelhouse, and it bridges OBX Lot Solutions' seasonal holiday work into a premium Jamieson product.
- **Move:** become a dealer for one brand this summer; sell into fall.

### ● 4. Security cameras + smart home — *cross-sell*
- **Why:** US smart-home market **~$28–36B in 2024, growing 17–27%/yr**, with **41.3% of US households** already adopting smart-home tech. ([Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-smart-home-market-report), [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/u-s-smart-home-market-107731)) Cameras/smart locks/thermostats are natural attachments to the rental-tech package and to any home you're already in.

### ○ 5. EV chargers & standby generators — *demand yes, license constraint*
- **Demand is strong:** NC EV charging points grew **~39% YoY**; coastal NC strongly wants **Generac standby generators** (storms/outages), and installs **book up before hurricane season**. ([WCNC](https://www.wcnc.com/article/tech/north-carolina-electric-vehicle-demand-scale-technology/275-88725cc0-50b6-42d2-bfe8-83c826fff707), [Generator Supercenter](https://generatorsupercenterofthetriangle.com/prepare-for-summer-storms-hurricane-season/))
- **The catch:** both hardwire into the electrical panel, which in NC requires a **licensed electrical contractor** — which you are **not** (yet). ([NCBEEC](https://www.ncbeec.org/licensing/))
- **Play:** either (a) **partner with a licensed electrician** and own the mounting/config/automation + customer relationship, (b) focus on **plug-in EV chargers** (NEMA 14-50 already present = no license needed), or (c) pursue a **Limited electrical license** down the road (2 yrs experience, up to $60k projects). Treat as secondary until you pick a path.

## The wildcard — mobile boat fiberglass / gelcoat repair

You have **structural fiberglass skill** (drop-tower work, ride repair) — rare and directly transferable. The OBX is boat-dense, NC sun constantly cracks/fades gelcoat (recurring demand), and shops charge **~$110/hr** with small repairs at **$450–$500**. ([No Wake Marine](https://www.nowake.com/north-carolina-fiberglass-gel-coat-repair/), [The Hull Truth](https://www.thehulltruth.com/carolinas/823260-any-fiberglass-repair-guys-eastern-north-carolina-area.html)) A **mobile** fiberglass repair service is a high-margin niche with thin competition. Worth a low-cost test (a few marina drop-bys) — it could become a premium Jamieson-adjacent line that nobody else reliably offers.

## How the two businesses feed each other

The customer is the same: **property managers and second-home owners.** Sequence it:

1. **House-watch** gets you trusted, recurring access to a property.
2. **Washing + junk + window/gutter** become the recurring OBX Lot Solutions revenue on that account.
3. Once trusted, **Jamieson** sells the high-margin work into the same door: **Starlink, wifi, smart locks, cameras, AV, lighting.**
4. Property managers control **dozens to hundreds of doors** each — so every PM relationship multiplies both businesses at once.

Keep the brands separate (you own 100% of Jamieson vs. a third of OBX Lot Solutions), but work them off **one shared list of property managers**.
