Questions

What owners ask before signing on

Is a home watch visit a home inspection?

No. A home inspection is a licensed profession in North Carolina. A licensed home inspector evaluates the condition of a home's systems and structure and gives you a written opinion, usually when a house changes hands. A home watch visit is different: we walk the property on a schedule and record what we saw, read and photographed at the time of the visit. Our reports don't grade systems or offer opinions on their condition. When something looks like it needs an expert eye, we say so plainly and help you get the right licensed trade out to the house.

How is this different from my property manager?

A rental manager looks after guest turnovers and bookings; between rentals, nobody may enter the house for weeks. Home watch is about the building itself (systems, water, humidity, storm damage) on a schedule you set, whether or not the house is rented.

Do you hold a key?

Yes, with your written authorization. Keys and codes are logged and kept locked away, never left in a lockbox on the door. You can revoke access at any time and we'll return everything.

What happens if you find a problem?

You get a call the same day with photos, an explanation, and options. Nothing gets fixed or spent without your approval, except what it takes to make the property safe, like closing the valve on a leaking line, which we'll do immediately and tell you about.

Will this satisfy my insurance company?

We can't tell you what your policy covers. Only your agent can. What we can say is that vacancy and unoccupancy clauses are common, that freeze/water exclusions often hinge on whether the heat was maintained or the water was shut off, and that carriers ask for dated records. Send us your policy's check interval and we'll set the schedule to match, then keep the documentation on file for you.

Is there a long contract?

No. Plans are month to month. If we're not earning it, you stop.
