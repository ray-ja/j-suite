Soft wash vs. pressure wash

The right method for each surface

Soft washing uses low pressure plus a cleaning solution to kill algae, mildew, and mold at the root. It's what we use on roofs, siding, screens, and painted surfaces — anything high pressure would damage — and it keeps surfaces clean longer.

Pressure washing uses high-pressure water for concrete: driveways, sidewalks, patios, and dumpster pads. We never pressure-wash shingles or siding — that strips and forces water where it shouldn't go.

Every wash includes plant protection, spot-treating stains, a full rinse, and cleanup. We leave it better than we found it and send you the photos.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the difference between soft washing and pressure washing?

Soft washing uses low pressure plus a cleaning solution for roofs, siding, and screens. Pressure washing uses high-pressure water for concrete like driveways. We use the right method for each surface.

Will washing damage my roof or siding?

No. We never pressure-wash roofs or siding — that strips shingles and forces water behind siding. Those get a gentle, safe soft wash that lasts longer.

How often should I wash my house on the Outer Banks?

About once a year for most coastal homes, since salt and humidity grow algae fast. Oceanfront and heavily shaded homes may need it twice a year.

How much does a house wash cost?

House soft washes start around $299 (more for two-story), driveways run $99 to $199, and roof soft washes start around $399. Free written quotes.

Do you protect plants and landscaping?

Yes. Every wash includes wetting down and protecting plants before and after, spot-treating stains, a full rinse, and cleanup.
