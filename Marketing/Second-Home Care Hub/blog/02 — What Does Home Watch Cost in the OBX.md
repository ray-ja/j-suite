## H1: What Does Home Watch Cost on the Outer Banks?

If you own an Outer Banks home you can't check on regularly, you've probably wondered what it costs to have someone do it for you. It's a fair question with a frustrating answer online, because most sites dance around it. We do this work locally, so we'll give you the straight version: what home watch generally costs, what moves the price, and how to tell whether it's worth it for your situation.

### The short answer

Home-watch visits are typically priced **per visit**, and the rate usually depends on how often you want them. On the Outer Banks, expect roughly:

- **Monthly visits:** around $50 per visit
- **Bi-weekly visits:** around $45 per visit
- **Weekly visits:** around $40 per visit
- **One-off / on-demand checks:** around $75
- **Pre/post-storm checks:** around $95

More frequent visits cost less *per visit* because the provider can route them efficiently. So a bi-weekly plan at ~$45 runs about $90/month, and weekly at ~$40 runs about $160/month. (These are typical local ranges — your exact price depends on the factors below.)

### What affects the price

Not every home-watch visit is the same, and a few things move the number:

- **Frequency.** The biggest lever — weekly is cheaper per visit than a one-off.
- **Property size and complexity.** A 6-bedroom oceanfront with a pool and hot tub takes longer to walk than a 2-bedroom cottage.
- **Drive time / location.** Remote stretches (think Carova, far Hatteras) can add to the cost.
- **What's included.** A basic walk-through is one thing; adding package handling, contractor meet-ins, or utility on/off for guests is another.
- **Reporting.** A quality provider sends a photo or text report every visit — that documentation is part of what you're paying for, and it's worth insisting on.

### What a good home-watch visit includes

Price only means something against what you get. A proper visit should cover:

- A full **interior and exterior walk-through**
- Checks for **leaks, pests, storm damage, and break-ins**
- **Running water, flushing toilets, confirming the HVAC** is holding temperature and humidity
- A **dated photo/text report** sent to you after every visit

That last point matters more than owners realize — not just for peace of mind, but because [many insurers expect documented checks on a vacant home], and a dated record is what protects a future claim.

### Is it worth it?

Here's the honest framework. Home watch is clearly worth it if your home sits empty for long stretches, if your insurer expects checks, if you don't have a reliable neighbor nearby, or if you've already been burned by a problem caught too late. It might *not* be necessary if you visit often, have a genuinely dependable neighbor who'll really check, or have a simple, low-risk property. (We go deeper on that decision in [Should You Hire a Home-Watch Service?].)

The math usually tips fast, though. A bi-weekly plan runs roughly $90 a month. A single undetected water leak, a freeze that bursts a pipe, or a mold problem that grew unwatched can cost tens of thousands and a ruined rental season. Home watch is one of the cheapest forms of protection an absentee owner can buy — it's the difference between catching a small problem and discovering a disaster.
