## H1: The Snowbird's OBX Checklist: Closing Up Your Coastal Home for the Season

When it's time to leave the Outer Banks for the season, the difference between coming back to a home that's exactly as you left it and coming back to a problem is about an hour of work on the way out the door. Use this checklist before you go — it covers the things that actually matter for a coastal home sitting empty through the winter.

### Plumbing & water (the highest-stakes section)

Water does more damage to empty homes than anything else, so start here.

- [ ] **Shut off the water at the main** if no one will be using the home. A burst line you're not there to catch is the nightmare scenario — and shutting off the main removes the risk.
- [ ] **Drain lines or maintain heat** to prevent freezing (yes, the OBX freezes). If you're keeping water on, keep the heat at a safe minimum.
- [ ] **Open cabinet doors** under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes.
- [ ] **Empty and prop the dishwasher and washing machine** to prevent mustiness and standing water.

### Climate & humidity

- [ ] **Don't shut the HVAC off** — set it to maintain a safe temperature *and* humidity (generally keep humidity under ~60% to prevent mold; see our [off-season mold guide]).
- [ ] **Set heat to at least ~55°F** if leaving water on, to protect pipes.
- [ ] **Place a dehumidifier** in any damp-prone or closed-off areas.
- [ ] **Leave interior doors open** so air circulates instead of stagnating.

### Food & pests

- [ ] **Clear out the fridge and pantry** — anything that can spoil, attract pests, or smell.
- [ ] **Empty all trash** and take it out.
- [ ] **Run a pest-prevention pass** — seal entry points; consider a treatment before you leave (quiet, empty homes are an invitation).

### Security & exterior

- [ ] **Secure or store outdoor furniture, grills, and beach gear** — winter storms move things.
- [ ] **Check window and door locks**; confirm any smart locks are set and old guest codes cleared.
- [ ] **Set timers or smart lighting** so the home doesn't look obviously empty.
- [ ] **Confirm gutters are clear** so winter rain drains away from the foundation.
- [ ] **Check seals and weatherstripping** before winter storms test them.

### Systems & safety

- [ ] **Test smoke and CO detectors**; replace batteries.
- [ ] **Unplug nonessential electronics** to protect against surges.
- [ ] **Leave a key and your contact info** with someone local you trust.
- [ ] **Confirm your insurance** covers an extended vacancy (this matters more than most owners realize — see our [vacancy insurance guide]).

### The part a checklist can't do

Here's the honest limit of any closing-up checklist: it's a snapshot. It gets the home safe on the day you leave. But the season is long, and things happen in empty houses — a heating system fails, a slow leak starts, a storm rolls through in January. A checklist can't watch the house for the four or five months you're gone.

That's the gap a **home-watch service** fills, and it's why so many OBX snowbirds use one. Scheduled checks through the off-season — with a photo report each visit — mean a problem gets caught in week one instead of discovered when you return.
