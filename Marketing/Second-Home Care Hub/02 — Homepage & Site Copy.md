# 2. STORM PREP FOR YOUR SECOND HOME

- **Slug:** `/storm-prep-second-home`
- **Meta title:** Outer Banks Storm Prep for Second-Home Owners (From Anywhere)
- **Meta description:** How to storm-prep your Outer Banks second home when you can't be there. A practical, coastal-specific guide to protecting a vacant home before the next storm.
- **Target keywords:** outer banks storm prep, second home storm preparation, prepare vacation home for storm, protect coastal home from storm, storm prep absentee owner

### H1: Storm Prep for a Home You Can't Get To

**Intro:** When a storm is headed for the Outer Banks and your home is hours away, the worst feeling is helplessness. The good news: most storm damage to second homes is preventable with a plan made *before* the season — and a few things you can trigger remotely or hand to someone local. Here's how to protect your place from anywhere.

**Section — Before the season (do these once a year):**
- Trim trees and secure anything that can become a projectile.
- Know your roof's condition — a pre-season inspection catches the weak spots wind finds first.
- Photograph the property inside and out for your insurance records.
- Make a plan for who secures the house if a storm comes while you're away. *(This is where a local home-watch service earns its keep — [internal link: Should You Hire a Home-Watch Service?])*

**Section — When a storm is forecast:**
- Bring in or secure outdoor furniture, grills, and décor.
- Confirm shutters/coverings are in place.
- Shut off water at the main if the home will be empty (a burst line during an outage is a top claim).
- Check that sump pumps and any battery backups are working.

**Section — Monitoring it remotely:**
A few inexpensive devices let you watch your home through a storm from anywhere — water-leak sensors, a battery-backed camera, a smart power monitor that tells you if the lights went out. *[Affiliate insertion: link to the Smart Monitoring page + 2–3 specific picks.]*

**Section — After the storm:**
- Get eyes on the property fast — damage caught early is cheaper and better for your claim.
- Document everything before cleanup.
- Clear debris before it causes drainage or pest problems.

### H1: Insurance and the Empty Coastal Home: What Every Owner Should Check

**Intro:** Here's something that catches a lot of second-home owners off guard: many insurance policies treat a home left empty for an extended stretch differently than one that's lived in — and some expect the property to be checked on a regular basis while it's vacant. Get this wrong, and a water-damage or vandalism claim can be reduced or denied. Here's how it generally works, and what to do about it.

> **Important:** This is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Every carrier words its policy differently — confirm the specifics with your own agent. (We'll even tell you the exact questions to ask, below.)

**Section — Why vacant homes are treated differently:** insurers price risk, and an unwatched home is higher risk — a small leak or freeze becomes a catastrophe when no one's there to catch it.

**Section — Common policy concepts to know:** vacancy clauses, occupancy definitions, and why "checked regularly" shows up in many policies. Plain-English explanations.

**Section — The questions to ask your agent:** a short, copy-pasteable list owners can take straight to their insurer (e.g., "How long can my home be unoccupied before coverage changes?" "Do you require periodic checks, and what counts as documentation?").

**Section — How documentation protects you:** a dated, photo-documented record that someone responsible was there and the home was sound is often the difference between a smooth claim and a fight. *[Soft tie-in: this is exactly what a home-watch visit produces.]*
