# OBX Second-Home Care Hub — Positioning & Voice

*The strategy layer for the Hub. Read this before writing or designing anything for the site. It defines who we're talking to, what the site is (and isn't), how it makes money, and exactly how it should sound. Everything else in this folder follows from here.*

---

## What the Hub is

A **content + resource site for people who own an Outer Banks home they don't live in full-time.** It answers the real questions absentee owners search for — storm prep, insurance vacancy rules, off-season care, whether to hire a home-watch service, smart monitoring — and in doing so it does three jobs at once:

1. **Earns trust** by being genuinely the most useful resource an OBX second-home owner can find — no fluff, no filler.
2. **Earns affiliate revenue** by recommending the gear we'd actually put in a coastal home (leak sensors, smart thermostats, cameras, generators, etc.).
3. **Generates leads** for OBX Lot Solutions' home-watch and property-care services — the site is the top of the funnel.

It is deliberately **separate from the OBX Lot Solutions brand site.** The Hub is an editorial, trustworthy *resource* (think "the guide a smart neighbor wrote"), not a sales site. That separation is what makes the recommendations and the eventual service pitch credible. The service connection is present but soft — earned, not shoved.

## What the Hub is NOT

- Not a brochure for OBX Lot Solutions (that's the brand site).
- Not thin affiliate spam stuffed with links — that destroys the trust the whole model depends on.
- Not generic national home-maintenance content — its entire edge is being **OBX-specific and absentee-owner-specific.**

## The audience: the absentee OBX owner

Roughly 60% of OBX homes are owned by people who don't live here. Our reader is one of them. Picture them clearly:

- **Lives somewhere else** — Richmond, Raleigh, DC, Ohio, often 3+ hours away.
- **Owns a place they love but can't watch** — a second home, an inherited family cottage, or a vacation rental they manage from afar.
- **Their dominant emotion is low-grade worry.** Is the roof okay? Did that storm do anything? Is a pipe leaking right now while I'm sitting at my desk 300 miles away?
- **They're capable and often affluent** — they don't need hand-holding, they need *trustworthy, specific answers* and *people they can rely on.*
- **They've usually been burned** — by a no-show contractor, a surprise repair bill, or an insurance headache. Reliability is what they're starving for.

**What they're really buying, across everything:** peace of mind. Every page should reduce their worry and increase their sense of control.

### What they search for (the keyword mindset)
They type things like: *"do I need to check on a vacant home for insurance,"* *"outer banks hurricane prep for second home,"* *"should I hire a home watch service,"* *"best leak detector for a second home,"* *"how to winterize a coastal house I don't live in."* The content is built around these real queries (full keyword targets in the site-copy doc).

## Voice & tone

The Hub sounds like **a knowledgeable local who happens to be a great writer** — the friend who actually knows coastal homes and tells you the truth, including when *not* to spend money.

- **Calm, authoritative, reassuring.** We lower the reader's anxiety. We never fear-monger to sell — we inform, and the right action becomes obvious.
- **Specific and local.** "Salt air corrodes coastal HVAC coils faster than inland" beats "maintain your HVAC." Specifics are the whole credibility play.
- **Honest to a fault.** We'll say "you can do this yourself" and "this $30 sensor matters more than the $300 one." That honesty is what earns the affiliate click and the service lead later.
- **Plain-spoken, never jargon-y.** Explain the *why* without talking down.
- **Warm but not chummy.** Professional neighbor, not a hype marketer.

**Words we lean on:** protect, peace of mind, from anywhere, while you're away, coastal, salt and humidity, documented, reliable, worth it / not worth it, here's the honest answer.
**Words/moves we avoid:** hard-sell urgency ("ACT NOW"), fear-mongering, vague national filler, keyword-stuffed robotic phrasing, exclamation-point hype.

### Voice litmus test
Before publishing any line, ask: *Would a smart, slightly worried owner sitting 300 miles away feel calmer and more in control after reading this?* If not, rewrite.

## How the three revenue jobs stay in balance

The trust comes first — always. The model only works in this order:

**Be the most useful → readers trust us → they buy the gear we recommend (affiliate) AND they hire us when they realize they need boots on the ground (leads).**

If any single page reads like it exists to sell, it has failed. Helpful first, monetized second, every time.
