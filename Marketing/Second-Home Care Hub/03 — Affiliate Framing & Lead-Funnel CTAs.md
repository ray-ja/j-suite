## PART A — Affiliate framing

### The governing principle
**We only recommend what we'd put in our own coastal home.** Every pick is chosen on merit, the reasoning is shown, and we say plainly when the cheap option is the right one. This honesty isn't a constraint — it's the entire reason anyone will click. A second-home owner has been burned before; the site that tells them "skip this, buy that" is the one they trust and return to.

### Disclosure (required, builds trust)
- **Footer (every page):** "Some links are affiliate links — if you buy through them we may earn a small commission at no cost to you. We only recommend gear we'd put in our own coastal homes."
- **Inline (on any page with picks):** a one-line disclosure near the first link. Clear disclosure is FTC-expected *and* it reads as confidence, not apology.

### How product recommendations are presented
Use a consistent **"pick card"** pattern Dev can template, never a wall of links:

> **[Product name] — *Best for [use case]***
> One honest sentence on what it is and why it's here.
> *Why we like it:* 1–2 specifics (coastal-relevant).
> *Worth knowing:* the honest caveat or limitation.
> **[Check current price →]** *(affiliate link)*

Pattern rules:
- **2–3 picks per category, max** — a "budget pick" and a "best overall," sometimes a "premium." Too many choices kills conversions and trust.
- **Always pair a pick with the guidance around it** — the product lives inside helpful content, never in a bare list.
- **Lead with the cheap-but-critical items** (leak sensors) — high trust, high conversion, genuinely the right first buy.

### The dual-monetization move (Hub's unique edge)
For higher-ticket or reliability-critical gear (cameras, locks, Starlink, whole-home networking), give the reader **two honest paths**: (1) buy it yourself via our affiliate link, or (2) have it installed right by **Jamieson Automation**. This captures revenue whether they DIY or hire — and it's genuinely helpful, because "buy this" and "or have a pro do it" are both real answers.

## PART B — Lead-funnel CTA copy

The Hub's #1 owned asset is the **email list.** Affiliate clicks leave the site; an email subscriber can be nurtured into a home-watch client (high-value, recurring). So the funnel is: **useful content → email capture (checklist) → House-Watch email sequence → home-watch client**, with service CTAs sprinkled where intent is highest.

### The funnel at a glance
1. **Capture** — the free Home-Watch Checklist (lead magnet) is offered on every page.
2. **Nurture** — subscribers enter the existing 4-email **House-Watch Lead-Magnet Sequence** (already drafted in `Marketing/Email Sequences/`).
3. **Convert** — soft service CTAs on high-intent pages route to OBX Lot Solutions home-watch.

### Primary lead-capture (checklist) — copy variants
**Hero button:** Get the free Home-Watch Checklist →
**Lead band headline:** The one thing every absentee owner should have.
**Lead band body:** Our free OBX Absentee Owner's Home-Watch Checklist — what to check on a vacant coastal home, how often, and the storm-season extras that matter. Yours free.
**Form CTA:** Send me the checklist
**Microcopy:** No spam. Just genuinely useful coastal-home guidance.
**Exit-intent / sticky variant (shorter):** Leaving? Take the free checklist with you. → [Email] [Send it]

### Service CTAs (home-watch) — by page, matched to intent
- **Storm Prep:** "Can't be here when the storm clears? We document and check OBX homes after every system. → See home-watch options"
- **Insurance & Vacancy:** "Want documented checks that satisfy most insurers? → See how OBX home-watch works"
- **Off-Season Care:** "Not driving in every few weeks all winter? We can. → See home-watch options"
- **Hurricane Checklist:** "Want a local team on standby for the season? → See storm-season home-watch"
- **Should You Hire a Home-Watch Service:** "If you decide it's worth it, here's how we do it — photo report every visit, across the OBX. → See OBX Lot Solutions home-watch"
- **Smart Monitoring:** "Smart monitoring tells you something's wrong; we're who responds. → See home-watch" + "Want it installed and reliable? → Jamieson Automation"

### CTA writing rules
- **Match the CTA to the page's worry** — the offer should feel like the natural next step, never a non-sequitur sale.
- **Soft verbs:** "see," "learn how," "explore" — not "buy now." This audience converts on trust, not pressure.
- **One primary action per page** (the checklist), with the service CTA as the warm secondary.
- **Always give a no-commitment path** — "see how it works," "free checklist" — low friction for a cautious, far-away buyer.

### Measurement notes (for Dev/owner)
- Track: email signups per page, affiliate click-through, and home-watch lead form submissions — so we learn which pages drive which engine.
- The checklist signup is the north-star metric; affiliate revenue is the near-term cash; home-watch leads are the long-term value.
