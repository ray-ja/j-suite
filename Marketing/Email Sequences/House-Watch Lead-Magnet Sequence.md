## EMAIL 2 — What insurers actually expect on a vacant home
**Send:** +2 days
**Subject:** The vacancy clause most OBX owners don't know about
**Preview text:** Your policy may require checks on an empty home. Here's how it works.

Hi {{first_name}},

Quick one that catches a lot of second-home owners off guard.

Most homeowners and dwelling policies treat a home left empty for an extended stretch differently than one that's lived in. Many carriers expect the property to be **checked on a regular basis** while it's vacant — and some can reduce or deny a claim for water damage, freezing, or vandalism if no one was keeping an eye on it. (Worth a two-minute call to your own agent to ask exactly what *your* policy says — every carrier words it differently.)

The reason is simple: the most expensive home claims — burst pipes, slow leaks, mold, storm intrusion — are cheap problems caught early and disasters caught late. A documented check is what stands between those two outcomes.

That's the real value of a regular home-watch visit. Not just peace of mind — a **dated, photo-documented record** that someone responsible was here, the home was sound, and nothing was missed.

On the Outer Banks, with roughly 60% of homes owned by people who don't live here, this is the quiet thing that separates the owners who sleep easy from the ones who get a bad phone call.
