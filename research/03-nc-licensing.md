# NC licensing — what he can and cannot legally sell
_Research run 2026-08-08 against primary sources: the official NCGS Chapter 87 Article 4 PDF, NCGS Chapter 74D as rewritten by S.L. 2025-51 (effective 2025-10-01), the codified 21 NCAC 18B rules from the NC OAH compilation, and the boards' own pages._

> ⚠️ This is research, not legal advice. The camera finding below is serious enough to confirm directly with the NC Security Systems Licensing Board before acting on it either way.

## 🔴 THE URGENT ONE — security cameras need a licence, and the Jamieson site advertises them

**NCGS 74D-2(a)(2)** covers anyone who "installs, services, monitors, or responds to … cameras, analytic capturing devices, systems providing intelligence or other imaging devices **used to detect or observe** burglary … theft, or other unauthorized or illegal activity."

- **"Or observe" means recording-only cameras are covered.** There is no monitoring requirement. Wireless and battery cameras are covered too.
- **74D-2(a)(1) also covers mere solicitation** — "advise, design, or consult on specific types and specific locations of security system devices." Quoting a camera layout is itself the regulated act.
- **Penalties are the harshest of the three regimes:** Class 1 misdemeanour (74D-11(b)) · civil penalty up to **$2,000 per violation** (74D-11(d)) · **the contract is void and unenforceable** (74D-11(f)) · and unlicensed sale/install is a **per-se unfair and deceptive trade practice** under Chapter 75 (74D-14), which carries treble damages.

**Exposure today: `jamiesonautomation.com` markets cameras on 20 of its 38 pages**, including a dedicated PoE-vs-WiFi cameras article, the homepage, the quote page and every town page.

**Getting the licence — there IS a course path, unlike electrical:** qualifying agent needs "necessary training, qualifications and experience" **or** the ESA **Certified Alarm Technician Level I** course (~22 hrs, online) — GS 74D-2(d)(3). Then $150 application + $38 SBI/FBI fingerprints + licence fee ≤$500 per 2-year term + liability insurance 250k/500k/100k (74D-9(d)), and every installer employee registered and fingerprinted (74D-8).
**The catch:** per NC DPS, a security-systems business must ALSO employ someone holding an NCBEEC SP-FA/LV or higher electrical licence for the wiring side — and that needs 3,000 hours. **So cameras need both boards.**

## The EV charger answer he asked for

**No, an apprenticeship is not legally required** — that was the wrong worry. The Limited licence needs **3,000 hours of experience, at least 2,000 of them "primary"** (hands-on installation of NEC-governed wiring). A registered apprenticeship is just one way to earn *secondary* hours; helper time counts at 80%, community-college NEC courses at 50%.

- **Cost is trivial:** $125 exam + ≤$100/yr licence. Under $1,000 all in.
- **Time is not:** 2,000 primary hours ≈ one year full-time installing wiring for a licensed contractor. Realistic floor **~18 months full-time**; part-time alongside the business, **2.5–4 years**. No course or fee substitutes.
- **"Limited" refers to project size ($60,000 per the current codified rule — third-party sites quoting $50k are outdated), not to a lower experience bar.**

**The one legal path without a licence:** GS 87-43.1(7) exempts "installation and servicing of cord-connected appliances and equipment connected by means of attachment plug-in devices to suitable receptacles **which have been permanently installed**." So mounting a plug-in EVSE and plugging it into an existing compliant NEMA 14-50 is exempt. Installing or replacing the receptacle, breaker, circuit, or hardwiring is not.
⚠️ **Ambiguity flagged:** no board interpretation applies (7) to EVSE specifically. Conservative practice — only where a compliant receptacle already exists, never open a panel or "check the wiring," and disclaim circuit adequacy in writing. Note NEC 625 and most manufacturers push hardwiring at 48A+, so this niche is real but narrow (≤40A).

## The GC threshold — his likeliest trap

GS 87-1(a) names **"grading"** explicitly, and the threshold is **$40,000** (raised from $30k by S.L. 2023-108, effective 2023-10-01). Bundled site-improvement work — grading, driveways, hardscape, drainage — counts as one undertaking including owner-furnished materials. **Splitting one job into sub-$40k contracts does not work.** An unlicensed GC also cannot enforce the contract or collect (*Brady v. Fulghum*, 309 N.C. 580).

## Holiday lighting — clean, with one real compliance cost

No licence implicated: cord-connected lights into existing exterior receptacles, far under the GC line. Adding a receptacle, a circuit, or a wired-in timer crosses into electrical work.

**OSHA is the real cost.** NC runs its own state plan enforcing standards identical to federal. Obligations run to **employees** — if Ray and Chase are both genuine LLC members with no employees, the Act doesn't reach them. **The moment a helper is paid as an employee, NC OSH applies in full:** ladder rules, and fall protection required above 6 ft in construction work. Roof work with an employee legally requires fall protection; that is the one genuine compliance cost in this business.

## What this means in practice

- **Sellable today with zero new licences:** everything he already does, plus networking / Starlink / AV / structured cabling (non-security), holiday lighting, and narrow plug-in-only EVSE.
- **Fastest new licence:** 74D security systems via the ESA CAT-I course — but hamstrung without the SP-FA/LV electrical companion (3,000 hrs).
- **The electrical licence is a years-long door, not a fee.**
