# Smart locks and rental cameras — the answer, including a correction to me
_Research 2026-08-10, primary sources: codified NCGS current through S.L. 2025-51, the enrolled session law, NCDPS board pages, Airbnb's live policy, an archived capture of Vrbo's._

## ⚠️ CORRECTION — my working hypothesis was WRONG
I suggested smart locks might be the unregulated alternative to cameras. **They are not.** NC regulates both, under the same chapter, and the 2025 rewrite tightened rather than loosened it. **There is no start-today-unlicensed version of either business beyond selling hardware.**

The strategic conclusion survives in modified form — but the premise I built it on was false.

## Smart locks ARE "monitored access control"
GS 74D-2(a)(2) covers anyone who installs, services, monitors or responds to *"…burglar alarms, **monitored access control**, or cameras…"*

"Monitored" modifies "access control," and the legislature supplied the definition by negative in the exemption: a lock is outside the Chapter when it *"does not transmit the data in real time to an on-site or off-site monitoring location."*

**Real-time transmission to a monitoring location is the line.** A RemoteLock / Seam / PointCentral / Schlage-Encode deployment pushes lock, unlock and code events in real time to a cloud dashboard a property manager watches across hundreds of doors. That is an off-site monitoring location. And the service itself — managing codes remotely, watching events, responding to lockouts — is literally *"installs, services, monitors, or responds to."*

**By tier:**
| Lock | 74D? |
|---|---|
| Standalone keypad, no connectivity | **No** (nothing is monitored) — but still locksmith work |
| Entry-logging, non-transmitting | Exempt **only in the hands of a licensed locksmith** (74D-3(a)(6) requires it) |
| **Connected / cloud-managed — the vacation-rental product** | **YES — inside 74D** |

⚠️ No NC case law or Board guidance interpreting "monitored access control" against consumer smart locks was found. This is a statutory reading. A written opinion request settles it: **ppsasl@ncdps.gov · (984) 220-8621.**

## And locksmith licensing applies too
GS 74F-4(5) defines locksmith services to include *"repairing, rebuilding, rekeying, repinning, servicing, adjusting, or **installing locks, mechanical or electronic locking devices, access control devices**… for compensation."*

**The hoped-for "new install vs rekey" distinction does not exist.** Swapping a deadbolt for a Schlage Encode is "installing." Penalty: Class 1 misdemeanour first offence, **Class I FELONY second offence.**

## ⭐ THE GOOD NEWS: one licence covers everything
**GS 74F-16(3)** exempts from locksmith licensing *"A person or business required to be licensed or registered by the… Board pursuant to Chapter 74D… when acting within the scope and course of the alarm systems license."*

**So the 74D Security Systems licence legalises the whole roadmap** — connected locks, exterior cameras, noise monitors, panel work — **and exempts him from the locksmith chapter.** One licence, not three.

**What it takes (and it is far softer than the electrical route):**
- A qualifying agent, 18+, character/background review
- **EITHER the experience OR the ESA Certified Alarm Technician Level I course.** The course alone satisfies 74D-2(d)(3) — **there is no years-of-experience wall.**
- $188 application + $550 issuance (2-year term); employees fingerprint-registered $88 each
- Liability insurance $250k/$500k bodily injury, $100k property
- **Processing typically 3-4 months**

**Why not skipping it matters commercially, not just legally:** GS 74D-11(f) — *"any contract for the sale, installation, or service of a security system shall be deemed **void and unenforceable**."* For a B2B business invoicing property managers, that is the decisive fact. You could not collect.

## Battery locks and the electrical question
A battery-powered smart lock touches no premises wiring, so electrical contracting does not arise. ⚠️ No explicit statutory carve-out for battery devices was found — this is the practical reading, not a citation. It only becomes a real question if he hardwires anything.

## 🔴 CAMERAS IN VACATION RENTALS — Ray's instinct was right, and understated
It is not a soft market. **It is prohibited.**

**Airbnb (effective 2024-04-30):** *"We do not allow hosts to have security cameras or recording devices that monitor indoor spaces in homes, **even if these devices are turned off**."* Outdoor and doorbell cameras allowed **with location disclosure**. Still banned outdoors: anywhere with a greater expectation of privacy — *"the inside of an enclosed outdoor shower or in a sauna."*
⚠️ **Nearly every OBX rental has an enclosed outdoor shower.**

**Vrbo:** *"Surveillance devices… can't be used inside of a property."* Outdoor permitted, security purposes only, location and coverage disclosed on the listing — *"It's not enough to include a photo of the device."* Violations bring full-stay refunds and suspension, and *"we'll enforce our policy even if local laws are less restrictive."*

**NC criminal law makes audio worse than the platform rules:**
- **GS 15A-287** — Class H **FELONY** to intercept an oral communication without the consent of at least one party. **One-party consent does not save an absent owner: when a camera mic records two guests talking to each other, no party to that conversation consented.** Any camera or doorbell recording audio in a rental is felony-exposure territory.
- **GS 14-202 (secret peeping)** — undisclosed interior image capture escalates to a Class I felony, plus a **civil cause of action with actual and punitive damages.**
- **Exterior video-only is lawful.** No NC statute prohibits it.
- **The NC Vacation Rental Act (Ch. 42A) is completely silent** — machine-scanned, zero occurrences of surveillance, camera or privacy.

⚠️ **Unverifiable:** Twiddy, Village Realty and Brindley Beach publish no camera terms at their public policy URLs (all 404). Do not treat any claim about their internal rules as established.

## What actually remains — and it is a better product
- **Noise monitors (Minut / NoiseAware class) are EXPLICITLY ALLOWED by both platforms** when disclosed and kept out of bedrooms and bathrooms. **That, not cameras, is the compliance product a manager can standardise across a whole portfolio.**
- Disclosed **exterior** cameras: driveway and parking — a real OBX pain point for occupancy limits and trailer/boat violations. Video only, never audio.
- **Where real OBX camera demand actually lives:** second and owner-occupied homes during the ~November-March vacancy, storm and hurricane watch (owners off-island wanting eyes on flooding and roof damage), construction sites, commercial. **That is monitoring of EMPTY buildings — no guest-privacy problem at all — and it pairs naturally with the home-watch brand.** (Assessment, not citation.)

## Bottom line
1. **Smart locks are still the better vacation-rental product** — platforms and managers actively want keyless entry, while interior cameras are banned outright. The market logic favours locks; the regulatory logic just says a licence comes first.
2. **One licence — GS 74D — unlocks the entire roadmap** and removes the locksmith problem. ~$750 plus course and insurance, no experience wall, 3-4 months.
3. **The camera market that is real is the empty-house market**, which is the home-watch customer.
