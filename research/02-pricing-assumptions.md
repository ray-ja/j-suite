## 1. Labour rate — $125/hr is the floor
CE Pro's integrator survey (~200 integrators): national average **$106.67/hr install, $114.28/hr service** (2023) → ~$117–125 adjusted to 2026. QuoteAV's trade guide recommends **$85–125 install, $100–175 programming/commissioning, $100–150 service**. NC electricians bill $75–125/hr.

The true cost of one qualified tech in an equipped truck is **$100–250 per SOLD hour** including overhead. At realistic small-shop utilisation, $125 nominal barely clears cost-plus-wage.

**The mistake would be one flat $125 for everything.** Network/Starlink/control-system configuration is the $150–175 tier; billing it at $125 is a giveaway.
_Note: the "undercut/value, never premium" rule was written for junk and land-clearing. Skilled low-voltage does not compete on price — different market, different rule._
https://www.cepro.com/business-support/research/ce-pro-integrator-wage-salary-pay-study-findings/ · https://quoteav.com/blog/how-to-price-av-installations · https://www.phcppros.com/articles/13328-should-you-charge-a-minimum-service-call-charge — **confidence HIGH**

## 2. The minimum — right instinct, wrong words
Standard residential practice is a **trip fee of $75–150** plus a 1-hour labour minimum; electrician service calls run **$100–200 including the first hour**. Four-to-five hour minimums do exist — but in **commercial/event AV**, which is B2B, not residential service.

His logic is sound: a minimum is "the smallest invoice worth dispatching for," and a bigger one pushes customers to batch small tasks into one visit — exactly what a long thin island needs. But a customer hearing "3-hour minimum" for a 40-minute TV mount reads it as padding. **The same $375 lands without argument as a flat menu price** ("TV mount + conceal: $349") or "$95 dispatch + $125/hr, 1-hr minimum" with a zone adder.

**Recommendation: minimum invoice $300–375 by zone. Never speak the phrase "3-hour minimum."**
https://www.phcppros.com/articles/13328-should-you-charge-a-minimum-service-call-charge · https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/electrical/hire-an-electrician/ — **confidence HIGH on practice, MEDIUM on the exact floor**

## 3. Drive time — track it, don't itemise it
Contractors who bill travel bill it at **50–75% of the working rate** ($62–94/hr for a $125 shop). Flat **zone fees** are the established alternative. Mileage-only at the IRS rate (~$0.725/mi) is explicitly warned against — it pays for the truck, not the lost billable hour.

**The OBX math that settles it:** Corolla→Manteo is ~1.5 hr each way. Three hours of round-trip windshield time at even 50% rate is **~$190 of real cost per visit**. That must be recovered — and customers accept it as geography ("Corolla zone +$150") while they fight it as "driving: 2.4 hrs @ $125." Every source stresses it must be disclosed at booking or it becomes a dispute.

**Recommendation: 3–4 published zones anchored on the bridges.** j-Suite keeps drive hours in their own bucket internally so the zone adders can be re-checked against actuals — which is exactly what the job-site address on a quote already feeds.
https://legalclarity.org/how-to-bill-for-travel-time-as-a-contractor-3-methods/ · https://fieldcamp.ai/blog/how-to-charge-for-travel-time/ — **confidence HIGH**

## 4. One price — his best-supported instinct
**~92% of homeowners prefer flat-rate** over time-and-materials; top operators lift average tickets **30–50%** on flat-rate. The reason given is always the same: it removes the "is he milking the clock?" fear.

Caveats: those digits come from flat-rate software vendors (directionally solid, methodology unpublished — don't quote the numbers as research). Flat-rate only works with honest hour estimates: **add 15–20% to your first-instinct hours** ("your instinct is almost always low") plus 10–15% project contingency. Genuinely unbounded diagnostic work stays T&M with a quoted first-visit cap.

**Benchmark:** Starlink installs market at **$199 (SpaceX's own rate) to $400–900** third-party. A flat "$499 standard roof mount + Ethernet run, Corolla +$150" sits squarely in market — and note the local competitor publishes $300/$500.
https://profitrhino.com/flat-rate-the-pricing-method-contractors-and-homeowners-prefer/ · https://www.satelliteinternet.com/resources/starlink-professional-installation-available/ — **confidence HIGH on practice, MEDIUM on the digits**

## 5. Hardware — the markup ladder, and the trap
Standard markup over dealer cost: **commodity (cable, mounts, connectors) 40–60% · mid-range (APs, switches, speakers, displays) 30–45% · big-ticket (processors, racks) 20–30%.** Blended project gross margin target **35–45%, never below 30%.** Integrators draw ~77% of revenue from equipment — underpricing hardware is how small integrators quietly starve.

⚠️ **markup ≠ margin.** A 35% markup is only a **26% margin**. CE Pro calls miscalculating this "a big industry problem." This needs to be enforced in the app's math, not left to mental arithmetic.

**The volatility is real right now:** tariffs have moved AV gear 10–46% by category; Crestron ran a 4% surcharge then permanent **5–15%** increases. So his "assume a price, win if it's cheaper" is the right direction with three guardrails: **quotes valid 30 days**, a re-quote clause for gear not yet ordered, and **order hardware the day the deposit lands** so the quoted price is locked when the risk starts.
https://quoteav.com/blog/how-to-price-av-installations · https://www.cepro.com/cep/big_industry_problem_miscalculating_gross_margin_vs_markup/ · https://www.commercialintegrator.com/news/crestron-and-other-av-vendors-to-roll-out-price-increases/147868/ — **confidence HIGH**

## 6. Consultations — the answer to the question he asked
**Free 15-minute phone/video qualification for everyone; paid on-site design survey $150–250 (zone-priced), credited 100% toward the job if booked.**

Contractors charging for estimates report close rates of **40–60% vs 20–30% for free** — the fee filters price-shoppers. At 20% close you drive to five sites per sale; at 60%, two. **Crediting the fee is standard practice** — it costs a serious buyer nothing and only charges tyre-kickers.

**Why the hybrid is forced by geography here:** a free on-site visit to Corolla is ~3 hrs round trip of unrecoverable cost — the worst free-estimate economics imaginable. The phone screen keeps top-of-funnel friction at zero; the paid survey means every truck roll is either revenue or a near-certain job. It also reframes the visit as a **deliverable** (a written design and parts plan) rather than charging to show up.

⚠️ **Honest caveat:** those close-rate percentages come from contractor-marketing content, not controlled studies. The mechanism is well-reasoned and universally reported; the digits are not verifiable. A paid survey **will** lose some price-shoppers — that is the design — so expect fewer raw leads.
https://www.jobnimbus.com/blog/how-much-should-you-charge-for-estimates · https://bwinsight.com/blogs/builder/the-500-free-estimate-trap-how-smart-contractors-stop-giving-away-their-expertise — **confidence MEDIUM (recommendation firm, numbers not)**

## 7. Utilisation — the number that decides whether $7k/mo works
Independent contractors work 45–55 hr/wk but **bill only 25–35 of them (~55–65%)**. Big dispatched field-service orgs hit 75–85%, but that assumes someone else does sales, quoting, procurement and admin. In a 2-man shop the owners ARE the office, and both drive.

**Plan on 1,100–1,400 billable hours per person per year.**

**The walk-through against the $7,000/mo goal:** $7k take-home ≈ $84k/yr; add ~$25k overhead (truck, insurance, tools, software, phone) and self-employment tax → **~$140–150k revenue needed per person** → at 1,200 billable hours that is **~$120–125/hr of LABOUR revenue alone, before any hardware margin.**

**So: $125/hr plus 35–45% blended hardware margin is what actually reaches the goal. $125/hr with hardware at cost does not.** (This walk-through is arithmetic on their formula, not itself a published benchmark.)
https://www.servicetitan.com/guides/contractor-playbook/hourly-rate · https://fieldedge.com/blog/technician-utilization-benchmarks/ — **confidence MEDIUM-HIGH**

## 8. Deposits — 50% is legal in NC and standard for special-order gear
**North Carolina imposes no statutory deposit cap** (unlike California's 10%/$1,000). The NC Licensing Board merely *recommends* minimal down payments. Norms: 10–20% for generic home improvement, **up to 50% accepted for specialised work requiring non-returnable special-order materials** — which spec'd AV/networking gear is.

**Structure it as:** 50% at signing, explicitly framed as "covers equipment procurement — your gear is ordered the day you sign." That (a) matches the accepted justification pattern, (b) locks hardware pricing against the tariff drift in §5, (c) means the shop never floats a customer's hardware on its own thin cash. For jobs over ~$10k, 50/40/10 (signing / substantial completion / final) is the mainstream pattern.
https://nclbgc.org/remodeling-home-improvement-contracts/ · https://workerscomplawattorney.com/contractor-down-payment-laws-by-state/ — **confidence HIGH**
