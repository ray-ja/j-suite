# Holiday lighting — the price sheet, and two corrections
_Research 2026-08-08. Market half; pair with `05-holiday-lighting-costs.md` for the cost basis._

## ⚠️ TWO CORRECTIONS TO THINGS I TOLD RAY

**1. "No competitor serves the OBX" was wrong.** Stateline Christmas Lights (Moyock) **explicitly lists Southern Shores, Duck, Kill Devil Hills, Nags Head and Manteo** in its service area — **but not Corolla**. The defensible claims are narrower and still good:
- **"The only Outer Banks-BASED installer"** — no island-based operator was found.
- **"The only installer serving Corolla."**
Stateline drives in from Moyock. Being local is still the wedge — but he is not entering an empty market, he is entering one served at arm's length.

**2. The $2–5/ft benchmark I quoted is the WRONG benchmark for his model** — and using it would have badly underpriced him. That figure describes labour-only handyman work on **customer-owned** lights. His model (installer owns commercial C9, cut-to-fit, maintenance, takedown, storage) has its own published trade band:

> **$8–12 per linear foot.** "Most professional installers charge between $8 and $12 per linear foot for roofline installation."

Corroborated three ways: ChristmasLights.io ($8–12, budget operators $4–6) · ChristmasLightsHQ (C9 $6–12, "quick answer $8–12") · QuoteIQ (full-service $5–8, premium multi-storey $7–12). HomeGuide's 2026 consumer headline has moved to **$5–12/ft**.
**My earlier suggestion of $500–700 packages was below the trade floor. Ignore it.**

## THE PRICE SHEET

### Roofline — year 1, all-inclusive (install + season maintenance + takedown + storage)
| | Rate | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Single-storey / ground-accessible | **$8/ft** | HIGH |
| Two-storey | **$9–10/ft** | MEDIUM — Thumbtack: "most installers charge twice as much on the second storey" |
| Three-storey / steep pitch / peaks & dormers | **$11–12/ft** | MEDIUM — Angi 3-storey jobs $700–1,800; +20% "dangerous job" adder |
| Deck railings (the OBX element) | **$5–7/ft** | LOW-MED — no published deck rate exists; nearest is fence-line $5–8/ft. Priced below roofline because it is installed *from the deck*, at ground-level productivity |

⚠️ **Measure every peak and ridge, not the footprint.** Peaks "can easily double your linear footage" — the most commonly cited way installers lose money.

### Packages (what the customer sees — almost nobody publishes a per-foot card)

|---|---|---|

### Terms
| | Recommendation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimum job** | **$1,000**, expressed as "packages start at $800" | ⚠️ His $375 / 3-hr trade minimum is **one third** of the full-service industry floor. Direct precedent: an identical-model operator publishes "minimum installation cost is $1000" |
| **Repeat year** | **Year-1 price −10 to −15%**, plus a 3–5%/yr escalator on the base | ⚠️ **This is his one divergence from industry norm.** Most full-service operators charge the *same* price every year ("you're not purchasing materials"). Published discount practice tops out at 10–15%. **Do not go deeper** — product is only 12–18% of a year-1 ticket, and coastal life is short |
| **Deposit** | **50% non-refundable at booking**, balance on install day · **10% early-bird if booked by ~Sept 30** | Multiple operators verified at 50%; none charge 100% upfront. December rush premiums of 10–50% are normal |
| **Property managers** | Per-home rate **−10% at 5+ homes clustered same week, −15% at 10+**; multi-year lock w/ 3–5% escalator; COI naming the PM as additional insured; one consolidated invoice | Published portfolio practice is 5–15%, not deep volume cuts. Route density funds it: 3–5 clustered installs/day vs 2–3 scattered |
| **Guest damage** (vacation-rental specific) | Maintenance covers product failure and weather; **occupant damage billed $85–125/hr** | Precedent: $85/hr after a free tier |

## Does it clear $125/crew-hour?
**200 ft two-storey at $9/ft = $1,800.** Year 1: materials ~$300 · install 5.5–10 crew-hr · takedown 2.5–5 · one ~90-min service visit → **~10–16 crew-hr → $94–150/crew-hr.**
**Year 2 at −15% ($1,530):** near-zero materials, faster re-hang → **~$150–250/crew-hr.**

**Year 1 only clears his bar at the top of the productivity range.** That is precisely why the trade says never start below $8/ft — and why **the repeat year is where this business actually makes money. The business is an annuity; the lights are the moat.**

## Productivity — a flagged conflict
- **15–25 ft/hr per installer single-storey, 10–18 ft/hr two-storey** → ~30–50 ft/crew-hr for a 2-man crew doing first-year custom work.
- One source claims 200–400 ft/crew-hr — **5–10× higher and unreconciled**. It only pencils for repeat-year re-hangs of pre-cut strands.
- Whole-job figures back the low number: 3–6 crew-hr for install *plus* takedown on a ~$950 job. Trees 1–2/hr, bushes 3–5/hr, windows 4–6/hr. Takedown is 40–60% of install time.

## Coastal practice — absorbed, never surcharged
Nobody publishes a coastal premium. What coastal installers actually do: dielectric grease in every female connector, sealed/gasketed sockets, nickel-plated bulb bases, no cheap steel clips, screw-lock connections, extra wind fastening, post-storm inspections built into the season.
**Decision rule: don't publish an OBX surcharge. Price at the top of the $8–12 band and use the shorter amortisation.** Christmas Designers, vendor-published: *"if you live close to the ocean, you can expect to reduce the life of your light sets by up to 50%"* → **amortise over 3–4 seasons, not 6–7.**

## The vacation-rental lane is genuinely open
**No exterior-lighting operator marketing to short-term-rental managers was found in any market checked**, and no OBX property manager (Twiddy, Village Realty, Carolina Designs, Brindley Beach, Sun Realty) markets holiday decorating. The closest analogue is Gatlinburg cabin decor at **$425–600+ per cabin**, marketed by managers as *bookable premium inventory* — never as a guest fee.
**Pitch: a bookability amenity for the Thanksgiving–New Year weeks.**

## And a free differentiator
**Every NC installer checked is quote-only. Almost no US installer publishes a per-foot rate card.** Publishing one is itself the differentiator — and it happens to match the undercut/value brand already chosen.
