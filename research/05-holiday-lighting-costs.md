## ⚠️ The number the whole model hangs on — and it is NOT verifiable
Pro-grade LED is rated **25,000–75,000 hrs ≈ 5–7 seasons** with proper off-season storage (drops to ~24–30 months if left up year-round). **But no published coastal replacement rate exists anywhere.**

Salt plus dissimilar metals causes galvanic corrosion at plugs and sockets — the mechanism is confirmed, the rate is not. **Recommendation: amortise over 3 seasons here, not 5–7**, and treat that as a deliberately conservative judgment call rather than a sourced figure. Mitigations that are real: dielectric grease on connections, nickel-based bulb bases, and gasketed/sealed storage rather than open tubs.

**Action: ask Christmas Designers' Pro Installer rep for a coastal replacement rate.** It is the single number that would most improve this model.

## Labour and productivity
- **Helper wage basis: $15–18/hr** (ZipRecruiter US average $17.39; most $13.46–18.99). Salary.com's $25–39 looks like job-title contamination — disregard.
- **Production per crew member: 2-storey roofline 10–18 ft/hr · tree wrapping 1–2 trees/hr · removal is 40–60% of install time.**
- Model job labour: 10–17 person-hr install + 4–10 removal + ~2 maintenance.

## The recurring costs first-timers forget
- **Storage: ~$20–60 per customer per year.** OBX units from **$39/mo in Kill Devil Hills**, $44/mo in Nags Head. Climate control matters here because of salt-air corrosion. (Per-customer figure divides a unit across ~30 customers — the unit price is sourced, the divisor is assumed.)
- **General liability: $400–$1,500/yr for $1M.** ⚠️ **Avoid any policy with a height exclusion above 15–20 ft** — that would void exactly the work he is selling.
- **Workers comp: NC only mandates it at 3+ employees**, so a 2-man crew is below the threshold — but that is a legal threshold, not protection from an injury claim.
- **Shrinkage: $15–40 per job** in lost/damaged product.
- Startup capital: **lean ~$1,675 / comfortable ~$6,100**. 28-ft extension ladder $449; fall-protection roofing kit $145.

## Two operational findings worth designing around
**1. GFCI trips are ~90% of callbacks**, almost always water inside a male/female plug connection at ground level. Fixes are cheap and preventable: elevate connections, orient sockets downward, dielectric grease, portable GFCI adapters. Doing this by default converts the biggest unpaid-callback source into a non-event.

**2. Favour C9 stringer over mini-light strings.** C9 wire cuts anywhere and re-terminates with a $0.75 vampire plug, and bulbs unscrew and transfer — so **inventory from a lost customer is largely recoverable**. Mini-light strings are sealed units and are the real stranded-inventory risk if the next tree is a different size. Since he owns and stores the lights, that difference decides whether losing a customer costs him an asset.

## OSHA
**29 CFR 1926.501(b)(13): fall protection required at 6 ft** for employees in residential construction. Applies the moment a helper is a paid employee rather than an LLC member. ⚠️ Whether pure portable-ladder work triggers it is genuinely nuanced and unresolved by the sources found.
