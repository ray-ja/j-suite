# Home watch licensing, and exactly when the camera obligation attaches
_Research 2026-08-10 against primary sources: official NCGS statute pages, NC DPS/DAC board pages, NCREC, NCBEEC, Dare County._

> ⚠️ Research, not legal advice. Two items below contradict earlier assumptions — read those first.

## 🔴 THE CAMERA PLAN AS STATED DOES NOT WORK

Ray's plan (2026-08-10): leave cameras advertised, do the ESA course when a job lands, first job pays for it. **It fails at two separate points.**

### 1. The advertising IS the first regulated act
GS 74D-2(a) opens: *"No person… shall engage in **or hold itself out as engaging in** a security systems business without first being licensed."*

The "personal solicitation" wording that looked like an escape hatch limits only the **selling** prong (a)(1). **"Hold itself out" is separate and broader** — advertising that you install security cameras is holding yourself out as engaging in activity (a)(2). The 74D-3(a)(7) exemption for "an entity through which a customer accesses marketing material" protects platforms carrying *someone else's* material, not a firm advertising *its own* installation service.

⚠️ Honestly flagged: **no board guidance or case law on the advertising line specifically was found.** The conservative reading is clear, and the fix is free.

### 2. The timeline makes "train when a job lands" impossible
Per the board's own pages, the chain is:
1. Qualify — 2 yrs experience in past 5, **or** the ESA Certified Alarm Technician Level I course. *(This part of his plan was right.)*
2. ⚠️ **THE PART THE PLAN MISSED:** the application requires *"a copy of your company's current North Carolina Electrical Contractors license."* **An NCBEEC SP-FA/LV licence must already exist** — its own application, its own PSI exam, its own experience requirement.
3. FBI fingerprints + SBI/FBI check ($38), $150 application, references, photo.
4. Board approval — **the board meets six times a year**, i.e. roughly every two months.

**Realistically 3–5+ months.** No customer's camera job waits that long.

### Act-by-act: when the obligation attaches
| Act | Regulated? |
|---|---|
| Advertising camera installation on the website | **YES — this is where it starts** |
| Site visit advising specific camera types and locations | **YES, unambiguously** — word-for-word 74D-2(a)(1). The visit IS the violation |
| Installing / servicing / monitoring | YES |
| **"Responding to" alarms** | YES — so offering keyholder alarm-response to home-watch clients is itself regulated. Keep it off that menu |
| **Referring the lead to a licensed installer, even for a fee** | ✅ **LAWFUL.** Hand over the *customer*, not a *design* |
| Subcontracting cameras under his own contract | **NO** — if Milepost contracts with the customer, Milepost is holding out regardless of who drills |

### The workable version
**Reword the site to cameras-as-referral** — "we'll connect you with a licensed installer," name a partner firm, take a marketing fee from *them*. Zero design or spec advice on site. If camera revenue actually matters, **start the licence chain now**, or take part-time probationary employment under an OBX alarm licensee (GS 74D-8 allows a 20-day probationary period) — which legalises hands-on work immediately *and* builds the 2-years-experience alternative.

### ⚠️ CONFLICT between two research passes — unresolved
Earlier research (research/03) cited **21 NCAC 18B .0805** as exempting low-voltage work from the electrical licence, making networking/cabling/AV his clean lane. This pass reads **GS 87-43.1** and finds **no low-voltage carve-out in the statute**, noting NCBEEC maintains the SP-FA/LV classification precisely because it treats this as licensed work.

Both can be true — a board *rule* can create an exemption the statute doesn't spell out. **But it is now genuinely unresolved, and it matters, because the whole networking business rests on it.** Do not treat "low voltage needs no licence" as settled. **Call NCBEEC and ask directly.**

## PART 2 — HOME WATCH: there is no such licence

**Direct answer: North Carolina has no "home watch licence."** Milepost can run licence-free — but **four regimes sit on its borders, and the marketing wording decides which side he's on.**

### What he MUST have
| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| **Assumed business name certificate** for "Milepost Home Watch" (Dare County Register of Deeds; one filing covers all 100 counties) | Trading under a name that isn't DYAD Holdings LLC — GS 66-71.4 |
| **Dare County business personal property tax listing** | Dare's own page: businesses "may not be required to have a business license" but ARE subject to this |
| **A phone call each to Nags Head, Manteo, Kill Devil Hills** if operating there | Those towns may require a privilege licence |

### 🔴 The trap: Private Protective Services (GS 74C)
GS 74C-3(a)(6) defines the licensed "security guard and patrol profession" to include anyone providing, for a fee: *"a. Prevention or detection of intrusion, entry, larceny, vandalism, abuse, fire or trespass on private property. b. Prevention, observation, or detection of any unauthorized activity…"*

**A paid contractor walking a vacant house looking for break-ins is literally performing (a).** Every exemption in 74C-3(b) was checked — none fits. (b)(13) covers only someone working "regularly and exclusively as an employee of an employer," i.e. a W-2 caretaker, not a multi-client contractor. **No PPSB guidance, AG opinion or case law carving out home watch was found.** Penalty: Class 1 misdemeanour, civil penalties to $2,000/violation.

**✅ THE FIX IS FREE AND IT IS WORDING.** Market Milepost as **property-condition monitoring and caretaking** — storm and water damage, leaks, HVAC, pests, humidity.
**NEVER advertise:** "security checks" · "patrols" · "we deter break-ins" · "keeping your home safe from intruders."
Reporting a break-in you happen to find is fine. *Selling* intrusion detection is the licensed act.

### The report format matters too — Home Inspector Act (GS 143-151.45)
"Home inspection" means *"a written evaluation… of two or more of the following components: heating system, cooling system, plumbing system, electrical system, structural components, foundation, roof…"*

⚠️ **NC's definition is NOT limited to real-estate transactions**, and the exemption list contains no caretaker or periodic-check carve-out. Penalty: Class 2 misdemeanour, each act a separate offence.

**✅ THE FIX, again free:** the visit report must be a **record of observations**, not an **evaluation**.
- ✅ "Water heater running, no visible leaks, thermostat read 68°F, photo attached."
- ❌ "Plumbing in good condition; HVAC functioning adequately."
- **Never use the words "inspection," "inspector," or "evaluation"** in the brand, the contract, or the reports.

### The other two lines
**Real estate broker (GS 93A):** only triggered by leasing, renting, advertising for rent, negotiating leases, or collecting rent/deposits. Coordinating a repair at the owner's direction is fine. **Never touch rent or security deposits.** The moment a client says "handle my Airbnb turnovers and guest payments," that is broker territory.

**Locksmith (GS 74F):** holding client keys and using owner-installed lockboxes is fine. Rekeying, repinning, fixing a sticky deadbolt for a fee, or bypassing a lock on a lockout is not — refer those out. Second offence is a Class I felony.

### NHWA accreditation — worth it
Not just a logo. It requires **third-party background checks of the company and each member, proper insurance, and bonding**. Since key custody is the entire trust problem in this business, "bonded, insured, background-checked, NHWA-accredited" is exactly the trust play already chosen for these brands.
