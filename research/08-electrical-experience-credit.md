# Does Ray's experience count? — YES, on the rules as written
_Research 2026-08-10 against the actual OAH rule PDFs, the ncleg statute, and the Board's real application package._

## THE ANSWER
**North Carolina does NOT require experience under a licensed electrical contractor.** The rule defines qualifying experience by the *nature of the work*, not the employer. His 480V industrial work and his low-voltage work both land in named **100%-credit** categories.

**The single real gate is VERIFICATION, not qualification.** The Board's form states flatly: *"Applicants cannot verify their own experience."*

## 1. No supervision requirement — verbatim
21 NCAC 18B .0202(a):
> *"Primary. As used in this Chapter, primary experience means working experience gained by the applicant while engaged in the installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code or work activities related thereto."*

The rule lists 16 qualifying **capacities**. The phrase "licensed electrical contractor" appears **once** in the entire experience rule — category (6), *"estimator for licensed electrical contractor"*. It is one category, not a general requirement. Nothing requires the hours be earned as a licensee's employee, under a licensee's supervision, or on permitted work.

Statute agrees — GS 87-43.3(a)(1): 3,000 hours, no less than 2,000 primary "gained by direct installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electric Code." **No employer condition.**

## 2. Who signs — any employer, notarized. NOT a licensed contractor.
The **Employer Statement Form**, bundled in the Examination Application:
> *"This form is to be completed [by] the applicants past or present **employer or employer representative under whom their electrical experience was gained**… The Board reserves the right to contact the Employer/Employer Representative and review all employment records… Separate forms should be used for each employer. Altered forms will not be accepted. **Applicants cannot verify their own experience.**"*

The signer must state a .0202 capacity (job titles are rejected — it must be a rule capacity), begin/end dates, total hours, sign with title, **notarized**. Plus 2 Character Statement Forms and $125.

**So: the amusement park can sign. A factory can sign. An integrator can sign. Ray cannot.**

## 3. His industrial work: 100% primary
.0202(a) names, all at 100%:
- **(10)** maintenance journeyman electrician / electrician mechanic in a full-time electrical maintenance department
- **(11)** electrical maintenance in other than a full-time maintenance department
- **(13)** *"electrical installations similar or equivalent to work performed by an electrical contractor"*
- **(14)** part-time or incidental work in any primary category

Wiring 480V three-phase motors, motor-control circuits and industrial panels is squarely NEC-governed (Articles 430/409). **There is no construction-only bias — the rule expressly embraces maintenance work.** His amusement-park hours are the strongest, cleanest part of the file.

## 4. SP-FA/LV — both earlier claims VERIFIED
21 NCAC 18B .0201(b)(5), verbatim:
> *"An applicant shall have 3,000 hours of experience… of which one year 2,000 hours shall be primary experience… An applicant in this classification **may also receive creditable experience for service in any of the capacities listed in Rule .0202 that the applicant gained in the low voltage field.**"*

- ✅ **Low-voltage field experience counts.** And .0202(a)(15) makes it a named 100% category: *"time spent installing or maintaining fire alarm or low voltage systems — 100."* Cameras, structured cabling, control systems, PLC/Modbus, Q-SYS — exactly this work.
- ⚠️ **NICET: credits time HELD, not a lump sum.** .0202(a)(16) credits *"time as a holder of NICET certification on NFPA 72 Level I-IV"* at 100%. A fresh cert helps credibility immediately but only accrues hours going forward. It is not 3,000 hours on day one.
- ✅ **STRUCTURAL PLUS:** unlike SP-EL, SP-PH, SP-WP, SP-ES and SP-SP, the SP-FA/LV classification has **no requirement to be on active duty in a firm "primarily engaged in" that business.** He does not need an existing alarm company to qualify.
- Special restricted licences have **no dollar cap** within their scope (.0801(c)). Limited caps at $60,000/project.

## 5. Own-company work — no exclusion exists
Read all of .0201, .0202 and .0210: **there is no rule excluding work on your own property, for your own company, or unpermitted work.**

GS 87-43.1's exemptions (own property / own employees) are exemptions from the **licensing requirement** — they say nothing about experience credit, and the experience rule does not cross-reference them. Legally-performed exempt work is still *"working experience… in the installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the NEC."*

**BUT** the no-self-verification rule means someone else must attest. For the escape room that means a **co-owner, partner or officer signing as "employer representative."** The form's own language allows a representative — whether the Board accepts one from a company the applicant part-owns is **genuinely unaddressed in any rule or form. Board discretion. Ask.**

## 6. The catch-all that was built for resumes like his
.0202(c), verbatim:
> *"Other Experience. The Board **shall** approve other experience that is equivalent or similar to the primary or secondary experience defined in this Rule."*

**"Shall" is a duty, not a discretion.** The Employer Statement Form has a matching "C) OTHER EXPERIENCE" block. Out-of-state hours are not restricted anywhere in .0201/.0202.

## 7. ⭐ THE STRATEGIC POINT
**If the amusement-park employment alone totals ≥3,000 hours (≥2,000 primary) and that employer will sign, he may not need the escape-room hours at all.** That is the cleanest possible file — a third-party employer, a named 100% capacity, no ownership complication. The escape-room hours then only need to top it up, and the SP-FA/LV low-voltage sentence plus category (15) lets them.

**What he must NOT do is sign a form himself. The form voids that explicitly.**
