# Review Request Kit — Templates & Cadence
*OBX Lot Solutions. Reviews are the #1 local trust driver — this is how we ask so it actually converts. Draft.*

## The rules that make asking work
1. **Ask every happy customer, every completed job.** The single biggest reason businesses have few reviews: they don't ask.
2. **Ask fast — same day, while the "wow" is fresh.** Conversion drops the longer you wait.
3. **Ask in person first, then follow up by text.** A verbal heads-up ("I'll text you a link") nearly doubles follow-through. **Text beats email** for response on quick asks.
4. **Make it one tap.** Always send the direct review link / QR — never "search for us on Google."
5. **Only ask people who are happy.** If they seemed unhappy, fix it first (see service-recovery note) — don't fish for a bad review.
6. **Never offer payment or discounts for reviews** — it violates Google's policy and can get reviews removed. You can ask; you can't pay.
7. **Reply to every review**, good or bad. Responses signal an active, trustworthy business and help ranking.

## The in-person ask (say this on site, at hand-off)
> "I really appreciate your business. If you're happy with how it turned out, the biggest help to a local crew like us is a quick Google review — I'll **text you the link** right now so it's one tap. Means a lot."

Then **send the text before you leave the driveway.**

## Ask cadence (the timeline)
| When | Action | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| At job completion | Verbal ask + "I'll text you the link" | In person |
| Same day (within ~1 hr) | Send Template A–D with the direct link | **Text** |
| Day 4–5 (if no review) | One gentle nudge — Template E | Text |
| Always | Reply to the review when it lands | Google |
| Ongoing | QR card left on site / on invoice / in email signature | QR |

**Recurring customers (home-watch/washing plans):** ask **once**, early (after the first great visit). Don't re-ask every visit — instead, after a save (caught a leak, storm prep, etc.), it's natural to ask if they haven't reviewed yet.

## Service recovery (when they're NOT happy)
Don't send a review link. Send:
> "I want to make sure this is right — tell me what's off and I'll take care of it." Fix it, then *if* they're satisfied, the ask becomes natural and genuine.
