# OBX Second-Home Care Hub — Audience & SEO Brief
*Research for Marketing & Dev. Absentee-owner niche site. Draft, cited. May 2026.*

## Who we're talking to
Absentee owners of OBX second homes / off-season rental cottages. Roughly **60% of OBX homes are absentee-owned** (internal Service Guide). They live out of state, can't eyeball the house, and carry real anxiety about what's going wrong while they're away. The Hub's job: rank for their worries, answer them, and convert to (a) our house-watch service and (b) affiliate gear.

## Core pain points (rank content around these)

**1. Burst / frozen pipes = catastrophic, silent damage.** A burst pipe can release ~**250 gallons/day**; just **1 inch of water can cause up to $25,000** in damage, and for absentee owners it goes unnoticed for days or weeks. Experts: keep a vacant winter thermostat at **55–60°F** and inspect at least weekly in winter / after storms.

**2. Mold from coastal humidity.** Moisture in a closed-up coastal house breeds mold. Mold reproduction effectively **halts below 50% RH**; ideal range **30–50%**. Owners fear coming back to a musty, mold-damaged home (and the remediation bill).

**3. Storm / hurricane exposure (Jun–Nov).** Nor'easters and hurricanes bring roof leaks, wind and water damage; coastal homes also fight **saltwater corrosion**. Owners want pre-storm prep and **post-storm damage inspection** fast — before small damage compounds.

**4. Insurance / vacancy compliance.** Many insurers **require periodic, documented inspections** on vacant or seasonal homes and may **deny claims** on losses that went undiscovered. This is a powerful, underused content + sales angle: "your policy may require this."

**5. Break-ins / visible vacancy.** An obviously empty house (piled mail, dark windows) invites intrusion and vandalism — owners want it to "look lived-in."

**6. Off-season rental gap.** Rental cottages sit empty Nov–Mar with no PM eyes on them; owners worry about the shoulder months specifically.
