## The rules that keep the crew profitable (read these — they came from real jobs)

**1. Size every job for two people.** Quote the effort Chase + Pierce can finish in the planned window. **Over the two-person ceiling → it gets priced to SUB, not handed to Ray.** A job that needs a third body, a skilled trade, or more than a long crew-day is a sub/refer decision — flag it to Ray at quote time, don't absorb it.

**2. Price by the JOB, never the hour.** Flat per-project number, always. Hourly gives away value and gets unpredictable for everyone. Customer script: *"Hourly gets unpredictable for both of us — I'll give you a flat number per project so you always know the price up front."* (This is a hard precedent — it's how the owner role pays off: value-priced job → crew earns the field share, Ray earns origination credit.)

**3. Travel / zone pricing — don't drive for free.** Quotes include round-trip mileage at the **IRS rate ($0.725/mi)** *and* windshield time.
- **Remote zones (Corolla / Carova / far north, ~35–40 min each way):** higher job minimum (**$150 vs the standard $100**) + a travel line, and **bundle remote trips** — never drive to Corolla for one small load.
- Build the travel line into the flat number so it's invisible to the customer but never eaten.

**4. One scheduled trip — extra trips are billed.** Set it up front with disorganized customers: one clearing/visit is quoted; repeat callbacks are a new charge. Repeat trips destroy the economics (the 5-visit job is the margin killer).

**5. Access assessment is part of every quote.** Stairs, multiple floors, long carry, blocked driveway = more effort → **access-difficulty bump.** Look before you commit a price.

**6. Everything runs through the business + the OA split — every time.** No customer pays the crew directly. Direct cash = lost tax reserve, no insurance coverage, no records, and it guts the split that pays the crew fairly. Non-negotiable.

**7. Equipment note:** the crew now owns the **STIHL AP battery platform** (~18" chainsaw + batteries) — so brush/tree quotes **no longer carry a chainsaw rental line** (still rent stump grinder / pole saw when needed). Straight margin gain on tree/brush jobs.

## On-the-spot YES / NO filter (for the crew, in the field)
A customer points at something and asks "can you guys do this too?" Run it through this in your head:

- **Is it on the SPINE or SECONDARY list, within the caps?** → **Yes**, scope it and add it (flat price, within guardrails; if unsure on price, "I'll get you a number today" and check with Ray — don't guess).
- **Is it OUT (skilled trade / big tree / pavers / needs a license)?** → **"That's not us, but I know who to call"** — note it for Ray to refer. Still a win: we stay the default call.
- **Does it need a third body / blow past a crew-day / need Ray on site?** → flag to Ray as a **sub** decision, don't commit.

When in doubt: **scope it, don't promise a price you'd guess at.** A fast quote that's complete beats a cheap quote that bleeds.
